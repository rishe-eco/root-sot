## 1. Purpose

Maintain helps a person recognize their own energy — across the three dimensions (physical, mental, spiritual) — and respond to it in real time. It builds *literacy in one's own state*, not a maintenance schedule. The metaphor is batteries: what charges them, what drains them, what each feels like near empty or overfull.

## 2. The shape

Three recognitions, all personal — Maintain teaches the person to read *themselves*, it does not impose norms:

1. **Recognize the experience of each battery.** What does high or low actually feel like, for this person, in each of the three dimensions?
2. **Recognize personal signals.** The individual's own early indicators of depletion and of overcharge — not generic symptoms.
3. **Recognize personal recharge strategies.** What actually restores each battery for this person, and what to do when one is running too high as well as too low.

## 3. Output: immediate action only

If Maintain surfaces a depleted or overcharged battery *now*, the response happens now. Deferring it to tomorrow is the app declaring it acceptable to spend another day in that state — which contradicts the pillar's purpose. If immediate action isn't possible, it leaves Maintain: a capability gap → Learn, a future commitment → Organize. No schedules, no recharge streaks.

## 4. The recognition-vs-action fork (and the risk it gates)

"Maintain" is an action verb, but its core is *recognition*. Held as pure recognition, it is the least at-risk pillar — but it then blurs into Reflect and over-promises against its name. Held as *sustaining action* (rest, boundaries, practice), the name fits and it stays distinct — but the acts can be tracked, targeted, and streaked, which reopens the door to overjustification. We resolve this by including the acts **but forbidding their scheduling**: Maintain responds to a recognized state in the moment; it never sets a recovery target or interval.

So Maintain's real risk is not quantification in general but **drift from recognition to optimization** — the day someone decides the energy-awareness feature "needs a goal." Guard against exactly that (see `02-anti-patterns-and-constraints.md` §3).

## 5. Relation to Reflect

Maintain and Reflect are both partly recognition and both immediate-action-only, so they sit close. The distinction: Reflect articulates *experience and meaning* (what happened, what it points to); Maintain reads *state and energy* (where the batteries are). When Maintain surfaces depletion, Reflect may be where the person articulates what's draining them; when it surfaces a motive question, that's Reflect's job. Keep the seam clean so features don't duplicate.

## 6. Decision test

Does the feature help the person *notice and respond to their state now*, or does it push them toward a maintenance regimen with targets? The first is Maintain; the second is off-pillar.

## 7. Open questions

- The battery metaphor's concrete form — how the three batteries are visualized/named per user without turning into gauges that invite optimization.
- Where the boundary sits between "spiritual battery" recognition and the deeper register of the philosophy (Core Philosophy §7).
