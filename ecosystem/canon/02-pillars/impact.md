## 1. Purpose

Others is the outward-facing pillar: the place where a person recognizes needs in the world they could meet, and where self-authorship stops being about the self alone. From Core Philosophy §4, it is the pillar that makes the rest true — the outward mirror without which self-knowledge is a hall of mirrors. From Brand §1 and the twofold purpose, contribution and self-development are one coupled task, not two.

## 2. The name (unresolved)

Working name: **Others.** Candidates and their trade-offs:
- **Serve** — the concept is right (service from compassion), but hides the relational dimension.
- **Relate** — reads well, but the *concept* (contribution/service) isn't visible in it.
- **Contribute** — holds both the act (give) and the direction (toward something larger) without the relational ambiguity; the current front-runner for the secular register.
- **Others** — accurate but flabby; also collides with ordinary "other things."
- Persian **خدمت** (*khidmat*) carries the exact register the sources intend and can do philosophical work the English can't. Decision pending.

## 3. In scope now: need recognition

The part we're building is **need recognition** — a discovery discipline in three moves:
1. **Understand the surrounding environment** — see the needs actually present.
2. **Evaluate self** — skills, abilities, and resources honestly.
3. **Recognize opportunities** — where 1 meets 2: needs you can currently care for.

## 4. Input pillar, not output

Others *identifies* where capacity could meet need; it does **not** execute. The recognized service act flows to **Organize** as a goal — but only after passing **Reflect's motive check** (compassion and genuine capacity vs. obligation and guilt). So the chain is: Others recognizes → Reflect audits motive → Organize executes with full rigor → Reflect+Organize handle any failure (effort vs. outcome). Discipline is the container for the compassion, not its enemy (see `organize.md` §6).

## 5. Why motive is load-bearing

The wellbeing benefit of service is **motive-dependent**: other-oriented motives predict higher wellbeing than self-oriented ones, and helping lifts wellbeing specifically when it's *autonomous*. This is the empirical spine under "service from the heart." It's also why legacy ("what I leave behind") is a byproduct we never aim at directly — the moment leaving-a-mark becomes the goal, the ego is back and the benefit evaporates. (Evidence caveat: much of this literature is correlational and skews toward older adults; treat "service improves wellbeing" as suggestive, and let the motive-dependence finding, which is cleaner, do the load-bearing work. See `04-research/`.)

## 6. Deferred: relationships

The pillar's second half — relationship management / quality / care — is explicitly **deferred (TBD, later).** The reasoning is contested even by the founder: close relationships may be partly handled through Reflect (need) and Maintain (energy), though those touch relationships only *instrumentally* and can't hold another person as an end with their own agency. Left open on purpose; not in scope for the first build.

## 7. Whitespace

No one appears to be building the service/contribution dimension as first-class personal-development tooling. This is genuine positioning whitespace, consistent with Root "making its own game" (Brand §3).

## 8. Decision test

Does the feature help the person *see honestly where they can contribute, from genuine capacity and compassion* — or does it instrumentalize service into a metric, a legacy project, or an obligation? The second is off-pillar.
