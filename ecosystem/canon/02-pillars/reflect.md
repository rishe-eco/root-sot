## 1. Purpose

Reflect helps a person articulate their experience, see what recurs in it, and act on what's alive right now. It is the pillar of self-knowledge through articulation — not a diary, not mood-tracking, but the deliberate turning of raw experience into something you can hold and understand.

## 2. The shape

Reframed from "understand your needs" to **articulate your experience and let patterns emerge.** Four moves, none quantified:

1. **Story first.** Not "what do you feel" but "what happened" — the day, an hour, a specific moment. Stories carry context; isolated feelings are noise.
2. **Feelings and needs within the story.** Once the story is down, name where feelings arose and which needs were met or unmet — anchored to the narrative, not floating free.
3. **Reality check.** Distinguish what actually happened (observation) from your reading of it (interpretation). This is NVC's observation-vs-judgment, used to audit your *own* articulation — not to impose a doctrine.
4. **Patterns (emergent, qualitative).** Over time, threads recur: certain situations trigger the same unmet need, certain words cluster. Patterns surface from the stories; they are never scored.

## 3. Output: immediate action only

If articulation surfaces a need that can be acted on now, it's acted on now. Otherwise it leaves Reflect: a capability gap → Learn; a future commitment → Organize; a recognized need in the world or a person → Others. No deferred tasks, no intervals. A Reflect that queues things for later colludes with the deferral instead of supporting the recognition (see The Loop §3).

## 4. Two layers, deliberately separated

- **Live reflection — the mirror (ships).** In-the-moment reflecting-back of what the person is articulating. Pure reflection: no analysis, no comparison to other entries, no judgment — and not mere repetition. This is the chosen model (mirror, not scaffold; cf. Seauton).
- **Pattern recognition — gated.** Cross-entry pattern-finding is powerful and *risky*: comparability pulls articulation back toward quantification (Etkin), and surfacing "you said X three times" can tip into rumination. It is a **separate feature, not built until professional psychological review** clears it. Its absence does not block the pillar.

## 5. Role in the couplings

Reflect is the **motive auditor** for service (Reflect↔Others): before a recognized service opportunity becomes an Organize goal, Reflect checks whether it comes from compassion and genuine capacity or from obligation. And on **failure** (Reflect↔Organize), Reflect holds the emotional and meaning-making side — what the loss says about what you value — while Organize holds the structural read. See The Loop §4.

## 6. Known risk and the bet we're taking

Reflect rests on NVC's claim that naming feelings and needs *deepens* connection rather than flattening it — a claim whose evidence base is thin. The better-evidenced Etkin finding runs the other way for *quantification*. Our position: articulation (translating a feeling into the right word and the need beneath it) is a different operation from quantification (compressing it into a metric), and the affect-labeling literature suggests naming is itself mildly regulating. We are betting on articulation-not-quantification, and we know it is a bet — hence the hard line against scales and the gating of pattern recognition. See `04-research/`.

**Sharpened (2026-07-22): the active lever is reconstrual, not writing.** Expressive writing's *average* effect is small; what makes reflective writing pay off is **reconstrual / self-distancing** — re-seeing the situation from a step back (Kross & Ayduk), not merely recording it. This tightens the bet: Reflect must be built to drive re-seeing (observation-vs-interpretation, the mirror that reframes), or it collapses into the low-value "just journaling" case — or worse, into rumination. In discovery this is **H6b** (writing alone isn't enough; reconstrual is the lever), split from **H6a** (unstructured in-head reflection tends to spiral). H6b is the product-level bet under the whole Learn→Reflect ordering; its failure would implicate that ordering, not just a feature. See `learn.md` §3 and `04-research/00-evidence-summary.md` §5.

## 7. Decision test

Does the feature help the person *see their own experience more truly*, or does it score, rank, or optimize it? The first is Reflect; the second is off-pillar.
