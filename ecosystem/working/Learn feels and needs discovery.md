# Journey — Learn Pillar, Module 1: Feelings & Needs Language
## Intended Outcomes & Discovery Foundation

*Internal strategy artifact — precursor to discovery interview criteria. Draft v0.*

---

### 1. Purpose of this document

This document fixes three things before we design the initial discovery interviews:

1. **What a person is meant to have *learned*** by the time this module has done its job.
2. **What marks "finished"** — which, as argued below, is a *graduation* state, not a completion state.
3. **What problems the module targets** — framed deliberately as *hypotheses to test*, not claims to defend.

It is not a spec and not an interview guide. It is the layer directly upstream of both. Everything here is meant to be converted, in the next step, into research criteria and Mom-Test / JTBD-switch questions. Because Journey is built through discovery — a growth-mindset, opportunity-solution-tree process that iterates until we find the path that both fulfils our goals and flourishes our values — the "problems" section is written as opportunities and assumptions, and the interviews exist to confirm, kill, or reshape them against real user behaviour.

---

### 2. Where this module sits

Journey's aim is self-authorship: the person is the author of their own life, and Journey scaffolds that authorship without ever taking the pen. That commitment is not a slogan; it constrains the mechanics (e.g. Reflect and Maintain emit immediate actions the person chooses, not schedules imposed on them).

Feelings-and-needs language is **the substrate literacy for that whole aim**. You cannot author a life from inner states you can't read. You cannot audit a motive in Reflect, or recognise another person's need, until you can tell a feeling from a thought and a need from a strategy in yourself. This module is first not because we had to start somewhere, but because it is where self-authorship physically begins.

Structurally the module is a **spiral, not a ladder** — one short recurring practice (the loop) that accretes two further layers on top of itself without ever replacing the base:

- **Tier 1 — the loop:** body-first noticing → name the feeling → link to a candidate need → optional small action.
- **Tier 2 — distinctions:** feeling vs. thought, need vs. strategy — surfaced *inside* the loop, on the person's own live material.
- **Tier 3 — storytelling:** taking a real situation apart (observation, primary and secondary feelings, multiple/competing needs, clarity). This tier is, in practice, a guided Reflect entry.

---

### 3. What a person has learned by the end

Learning outcomes are written as **observable capabilities**, because only observable capabilities are testable in interviews and evaluable in-product. A graduate of this module can, to a workable degree:

**Mindset (foundational, and the highest-leverage outcome)**
- Treat emotions as *malleable, informative signals* rather than fixed verdicts about themselves — the incremental rather than entity stance. This belief shift is the single change with the most direct evidence behind it, so it is a first-class outcome, not a side effect.

**Body literacy**
- Locate and describe the *bodily texture* of an affective state (tight, heavy, jittery, warm) before reaching for a word — using sensation as the on-ramp to naming, rather than jumping straight to a story about it.

**Vocabulary and granularity (Competence A)**
- Reach for a *specific, personally-used* feeling word beyond "good / bad / fine / stressed," and distinguish between nearby feelings (e.g. disappointed vs. drained vs. uneasy). The emphasis is on words they have *used in context*, not memorised from a list.

**Coupling**
- Habitually read a feeling as information about a *need* — "this points at something I care about" — so that naming a feeling and orienting toward a need become one motion.

**Distinctions (Competence B)**
- Catch a *thought-masquerading-as-a-feeling* ("I feel ignored / abandoned / disrespected") as a read on someone else's behaviour, and find the actual feeling underneath it plus the need it signals.
- Tell a *need* from a *strategy* — distinguish "I need reassurance / connection" from "I need you to text me back."

**Integration / narrative (Tier 3)**
- Take a real situation apart: separate what happened (observation) from their evaluation of it, name a primary and at least one underlying/secondary feeling, and surface more than one need — including needs in tension.

**Meta outcome — the self-authorship payoff**
- Run the loop *self-initiated*, without a prompt. This is the point at which the scaffold has installed a capacity that now runs on its own. It is both the deepest outcome and the clearest graduation signal.

### 4. What marks "finished"

**Finished is a graduation state, not a completion state.** Because granularity is built by repeated in-context labelling over time, there is no point at which the practice is "done." The loop is meant to become a lifelong micro-habit; the module's job is to make it *self-sustaining and Reflect-ready*, not to be exited.

A person has **graduated** when the substrate literacy runs without the tool holding it up — concretely, when they:

- run the loop unprompted, at least sometimes;
- reach for specific feeling and need words without leaning on the offered palette;
- catch at least some of their own faux-feelings and need/strategy confusions in the moment;
- have taken a few storytelling passes and found them *tractable* — i.e. they can walk into Reflect and do real work there.

Graduation is therefore best understood as **an open door into Reflect**, not a finish line. The storytelling tier and the entry point of Reflect are nearly the same activity, so a graduate does not "complete Module 1 and move on" — they walk through a door they are already standing in.

Every threshold implied above ("a few passes," "at least sometimes") is a **placeholder and a dial**, not a truth. Tuning these per user is precisely the job of the module's evaluation-and-settings layer. For the team prototype, hardcode them so the arc can be *felt*, and treat them as adjustable.

### 5. Problems targeted — as discovery hypotheses

These are the opportunities this module bets on. Each is written as an assumption about **real, present pain in the user's life**, because that is what the discovery interviews must test — through specific past and current behaviour, not enthusiasm for our solution. The "evidence signal" line for each names the kind of concrete, behavioural account that would confirm the pain is real rather than merely plausible.

**Note on levels.** H1–H5 are *user-level* pains — things the person feels directly in their own life. H6 is a *product-level* bet: it is the assumption that justifies the entire Learn → Reflect sequence. It is weighted more heavily than the others (see §6).

**H1 — Coarse emotional resolution.** People operate at "I feel bad / off / stressed" and lack the vocabulary to get specific; the vagueness itself is distressing and blocks action.
*Evidence signal:* recent instances of being stuck in a mood they couldn't name, and what (if anything) they did about it.

**H2 — Feelings confused with judgements about others.** People narrate interpretations ("I feel ignored / let down") as feelings; this embeds blame, fuels conflict, and leaves them feeling powerless.
*Evidence signal:* a recent conflict recounted in "you did X to me" terms, and how it resolved (or didn't).

**H3 — Needs confused with strategies.** People frame a need as a demand on a specific person ("I need him to call more"); when that one route is unavailable they are stuck, and the demand strains the relationship.
*Evidence signal:* a recent want fixated on one particular person/action, and what happened when it wasn't met.

**H4 — Emotions experienced as fixed and external.** People feel *at the mercy* of their emotions and don't believe they can work with them (the entity stance), which lowers their sense of agency.
*Evidence signal:* language of helplessness about emotions ("that's just how I am," "it takes over"), and whether they've ever tried to influence a feeling deliberately.

**H5 — Disconnection from bodily signal.** People jump straight to stories and cannot locate or distinguish the bodily sensation underneath an emotion, so naming is ungrounded or absent.
*Evidence signal:* difficulty answering "where did you feel it" for a recent strong emotion.

**H6 — Reflection without substrate becomes rumination (the product-level bet, weighted highest).** People who want to reflect or journal lack the feeling/need literacy to do it well, so the activity turns into venting or spiralling rather than insight. This is the largest bet, and the bridge to Reflect: *without this literacy, reflection tools produce rumination, not clarity.* Because H6 is the assumption underwriting the whole Learn → Reflect ordering, its disconfirmation would be a louder signal than any single user-level pain: if our three users do **not** recognise H6 in their own history, that is stronger evidence that the sequencing is wrong than that this specific module is. Treat a failure to confirm H6 as a prompt to revisit the pillar ordering, not just this module's scope.
*Evidence signal:* accounts of journaling / overthinking that went in circles rather than resolving.

**Anti-goal to guard against (a problem the module must not *create*).** Naming without forward motion can amplify distress — the notice → analyse → spiral failure mode. The loop is designed to move (notice → name → need → small action) precisely to avoid this. The interviews should stay alert to any sign that reflection-type practices have historically made a person feel *worse*, so we can validate that the forward-motion design is load-bearing.

---

### 6. Implication for the discovery interviews

The interviews are not testing whether people like the module. They are testing whether H1–H6 name real, currently-felt pain in the lives of our three known users — and which of those pains is sharpest, because the sharpest real pain is where the first build should aim. The learning outcomes in §3 and the graduation picture in §4 are the *shape of the solution* we are provisionally holding; the interviews exist to check that this shape is answering a problem people actually have, before we commit build effort to it.

**Weight H6 explicitly.** H1–H5 tell us whether *this module* solves a real pain; H6 tells us whether the *Learn → Reflect sequence* is built on solid ground. A user-level pain (H1–H5) that fails to confirm reshapes this module's scope. H6 failing to confirm reshapes the pillar ordering itself. Build the interview criteria so H6 gets deliberate, direct probing rather than being folded in as one hypothesis among six — its answer carries more architectural weight than the others combined.
