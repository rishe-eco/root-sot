## 1. What this document decides, and where it sits

The foundation doc fixed *what we believe* about Learn Module 1: the outcomes, the "graduation not completion" model, and six hypotheses about real user pain (H1–H6). This document turns that into a **research plan**: the explicit questions the discovery must answer, and the method — chosen, justified, and grounded — that will answer them.

It is deliberately upstream of the instrument. The exact interview threads / questionnaire items are **Doc 2**; who we talk to and how we screen them is **Doc 3**. Both inherit their logic from here.

In engine terms this is **Phase 1 — Establish outcome & map the opportunity space**, run on Learn as the pilot pillar (Learn has strong evidence anchors, which is exactly why the engine says pilot on it rather than on Others). The **outcome node is locked** from the foundation doc; the **opportunities are the hypotheses**; discovery either confirms, kills, or reshapes them against real behaviour.

**Grounding note.** The approach below is not invented for Root. It composes three established discovery practices (the Mom Test, Torres's story-based Continuous Discovery, and the JTBD "switch" interview) and sits inside a recognised academic method for building digital behaviour-change interventions (Yardley et al.'s **Person-Based Approach**, whose core move is *qualitative research with users at every stage* plus a set of *guiding principles* to test — our hypotheses are exactly those provisional guiding principles). Full references in §12.

**The development model this discovery serves (decided; it shapes everything below).** Root builds the five pillars as **independent, standalone apps**, one at a time, and **integrates them into the unified "Journey" app only once all five are shaped** — planned ~9 months out (a living estimate). An **integration vision is held from day one** so each pillar is designed to fit its relatives (the "coherence counterweight," canon `../../canon/03-engine/02-async-coordination.md` §6; `../../canon/02-pillars/00-the-loop.md` §7), but until integration there is no "Journey" — just five pillar apps. Two consequences steer this research:

> 1. **We build Learn Module 1 regardless of the H6/RQ6 result.** H6 was framed as the bet justifying the Learn→Reflect *ordering*; that ordering is now an **integration-phase decision (~9 months out), not a Phase-1 gate.** So the discovery's job is not "should we build this" but **"what exactly must this module accomplish *standalone*, and how do we make it excellent."**
> 2. **Graduation currently opens onto continued standalone use, not into Reflect.** The module keeps its graduation point (the loop self-sustains; users keep using it), but Reflect doesn't exist yet to receive them — the "open door into Reflect" (foundation §4) is a *future* door. We design toward it (the integration vision) without depending on it now.

## 2. The research questions

The "why" behind each is in the foundation doc §5 (the pains and their evidence signals); we do not repeat it. What follows is the operational question set, structured as the Opportunity Solution Tree reads it.

**Locked outcome (top of the tree):** *A person can read their own inner states — telling a feeling from a thought, and a need from a strategy — well enough to author their own life and to walk into Reflect and do real work there.*

### 2.1 Framing / demand questions (the JTBD layer — asked first, before any pain probing)
- **RQ-A — Is there a real "job" here at all?** When, if ever, have people actively sought a way to understand their own feelings — and what triggered that search? What did they hire (therapy, journaling, a friend, a book, an app, nothing), and how did it go?
- **RQ-B — What are the forces around that switch?** What *pushed* them (the pain of the old way), what *pulled* them (the promise of the new), and what **anxiety/habit** held them back? (This tells us whether the pain is live enough to move someone, not just real in the abstract.)

### 2.2 Pain-validation questions (one per hypothesis — the core of the phase)
For each, the question is the same shape: **Does this pain show up in specific, recent, real behaviour — not in the person's agreement that it sounds true?**

| RQ | Hypothesis (foundation §5) | The question we're answering |
|----|----------------------------|------------------------------|
| RQ1 | H1 — coarse emotional resolution | Do people get stuck in moods they can't name, and does the vagueness itself block action? |
| RQ2 | H2 — feelings confused with judgements of others | Do people narrate interpretations ("ignored," "let down") *as feelings*, and does it fuel blame/conflict/powerlessness? |
| RQ3 | H3 — needs confused with strategies | Do people fixate a need on one person/route, and get stuck (and strain the relationship) when it's unavailable? |
| RQ4 | H4 — emotions felt as fixed & external (entity stance) | Do people talk about emotions as things that happen *to* them, beyond their influence? |
| RQ5 | H5 — disconnection from bodily signal | Do people jump to story and struggle to locate the sensation under an emotion? |
| **RQ6** | **H6 — reflection without substrate → spiral** *(now split; role reframed — see §2.4)* | **H6a (standalone-relevant):** does unstructured in-head reflection tend to *spiral* rather than resolve — such that feelings/needs literacy would help in daily life on its own? **H6b (integration-relevant, deferred):** is the active lever **reconstrual/re-seeing**, not writing per se? Informs how to design the storytelling tier to fit Reflect *later*. |

**RQ-V — the module's own success (now primary).** Setting aside any future Reflect destination: *what must this module accomplish, standalone, to be genuinely worth using — and does its mechanism (the notice→name→need→small-action loop, built into daily life) produce felt benefit in the person's real life on its own?* Because Learn ships as a standalone app long before Reflect exists (§1, the development model), **this — not the Learn→Reflect bridge — is the question the build most needs answered.** RQ-V is answered by the same stories that answer RQ1–RQ5 and RQ-G: the pains those name are exactly what standalone literacy would relieve.

### 2.3 Prioritisation & guardrail questions
- **RQ-P — Which pain is sharpest?** Across participants, which of H1–H5 is most **frequent × most impactful × most currently-felt**? The sharpest *real* pain is where the first build aims (foundation §6).
- **RQ-G — Does naming ever make it worse?** Have reflection/naming practices historically left someone feeling *worse* (the notice→analyse→spiral failure mode)? This validates that the loop's forward-motion design (notice→name→need→small action) is load-bearing, not decorative (foundation §5 anti-goal).

### 2.4 What leads the build now — and what's deferred to integration
**Reframed by the development model (§1).** We will build Learn Module 1 regardless of the H6 result, because it ships standalone and there is no Reflect to gate against yet. So:

- **Leading now (primary):** the module's **standalone definition-of-done** (RQ-V) and the **sharpest standalone pain** among H1–H5 (RQ-P). These decide what we build and where it aims. **H6a** feeds this — if in-head reflection spirals, standalone feelings/needs literacy has direct daily value, no Reflect required.
- **Retained but deferred (the integration bet):** **H6b** (reconstrual is the lever) and the Learn→Reflect *ordering* it underwrites. We still probe H6b now — it's cheap while we have people, and it tells us how to shape the storytelling tier so the module *fits Reflect* when integration comes (~9 months out). But a weak H6b **routes to the integration backlog**, not to a stop-or-reorder decision. The pillar-ordering call is an **integration-phase decision, made when Reflect actually exists.**

Net: H6 moves from *gate* to *integration design-input*. The gate for *this* build is **RQ-V + RQ-P**. (Canon reconciled via the decision log, 2026-07-23; `../../canon/02-pillars/learn.md` §3 updated.)

## 3. Method — the how

### 3.1 Chosen method
**Qualitative, semi-structured, story-based discovery interviews**, one-to-one, ~45–60 min, conducted **in English for this first cohort** (see §9). Each interview composes three disciplines, each doing a distinct job:

- **The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick)** — the *discipline*: talk about their life, not our idea; ask for specific past events, not opinions or futures; never pitch or describe the module; let them talk ~80% of the time. This is what keeps the data honest.
- **Torres story-based interviewing** — the *elicitation*: open with "tell me about a specific recent time when…" and mine the story. Opportunities (unmet needs, pains) surface *from the stories*, in context, which is what lets us map them onto the tree rather than collecting abstract complaints.
- **JTBD switch / forces of progress (Moesta)** — the *demand lens*: reconstruct the timeline of the last time they sought to understand a feeling (first thought → passive looking → a triggering event → active looking → trying something → outcome), and read the four forces (push, pull, anxiety, habit). This distinguishes "real but inert" pain from pain live enough to move someone.

Analysis is **thematic** against the a-priori hypotheses *plus* open coding for opportunities we didn't predict; results are mapped onto the Opportunity Solution Tree (§5).

### 3.2 Why interviews, and why not a questionnaire (this choice is load-bearing)
The foundation doc and the user asked specifically why the method. The case:

1. **The deficit hides itself — so self-report can't measure it.** Several hypotheses are about a *lack of a capability the person can't see they lack*. Someone with coarse emotional resolution (H1) cannot accurately rate their own emotional resolution on a scale; someone who mistakes "ignored" for a feeling (H2) will, by definition, report it *as* a feeling. A questionnaire measures **self-perception of the deficit**, which is exactly the thing the deficit distorts. An interview lets us **observe the phenomenon in the person's own narration** — we hear the faux-feeling, the need/strategy collapse, the spiral — rather than asking them to grade it. That is a validity argument, not a preference.
2. **We want behaviour, not attitude.** Discovery is about *real, present pain evidenced in past behaviour* (Mom Test). Surveys are efficient at collecting opinions and predicted future behaviour — the two things we most distrust. Stated importance ≠ lived pain.
3. **We're discovering shape, not confirming a known quantity.** You run a survey when you already know what to ask and want to measure prevalence. We're still finding the *shape* of the opportunity space and its sharpest point — the exploratory, hypothesis-generating end of the spectrum, where qualitative interviewing is the right instrument.
4. **The subtle phenomena are only visible in narrative.** Feeling-vs-thought, need-vs-strategy, and especially the reconstrual mechanism (H6b) show up in *how someone tells a story*, not in a checkbox.

**Where structured elements still earn a place (deferred to Doc 2/3):** a short **screening intake** to select participants; and an optional lightweight **retrospective diary prompt** if a live example doesn't surface. *(A brief validated scale — emotion-beliefs or granularity — was considered as a screener/opener; **decision 2026-07-23: no scale for now**, keep it purely conversational.)* These complement interviews; they don't replace them.

### 3.3 What we will deliberately NOT do
- **No pitching, no demo, no describing the module** during discovery — the moment we show the solution the data is contaminated (Mom Test). Reactions to our design come later, in Phase 3 testing.
- **No Likert self-ratings as primary evidence** (see §3.2.1).
- **No large-N survey yet** — premature; it would measure prevalence of the wrong construct before we know the construct.
- **No leading toward our hypotheses** — we ask for the story and let the pain (or its absence) emerge; a hypothesis "confirmed" only because we fished for it is a false positive (see §7).

### 3.4 Fit to established intervention-development practice
This isn't just product-discovery folklore. The **Person-Based Approach** (Yardley et al., JMIR 2015) — a recognised method for developing digital health / behaviour-change interventions — prescribes exactly this: iterative *qualitative research with users at every stage*, going beyond usability to understand the psychosocial context, feeding a set of *guiding principles*. Our H1–H6 **are** provisional guiding principles; this phase is their first empirical test. Comparable emotion-regulation apps were built the same way — formative qualitative work and co-design before committing build (see §12 benchmarks). We are on a well-trodden path, applied honestly.

## 4. Evidence & decision criteria

### 4.1 Per-hypothesis: confirm / kill / reshape
For each hypothesis we look for the **evidence signal** named in foundation §5 — a concrete behavioural account, not agreement. Per participant, rate each hypothesis:

- **Confirmed** — a specific, recent, real episode demonstrates the pain (they *lived* it, we didn't prompt the conclusion).
- **Absent** — asked well, no such episode; the pain isn't there for this person.
- **Reshaped** — an episode shows a *related but different* pain than we hypothesised (this is a signal, not a failure — it edits the tree).

Then across participants: a hypothesis is **live** if it confirms in a clear majority with real episodes; **doubtful** if mostly absent; **reshaped** if the pattern that recurs isn't the one we wrote. Record the *episodes*, not just the verdict — the tree carries reasoning, not conclusions (canon OST §5).

### 4.2 Depth allocation (variable depth, per engine §4)
Because Learn has strong anchors, H1–H5 need lighter confirmation. Reserve the **most interview time for RQ-V (standalone value) and the sharpest-pain read (RQ-P)** — that is what gates and aims this build. Still keep a dedicated block for the **H6a/H6b timeline** (a real past reflection/journaling episode, walked in detail: did it reframe, or circle?) — H6a because it strengthens the standalone value case, H6b because it's cheap to gather now and shapes the eventual Reflect fit. Just don't let H6b — an integration-phase bet — crowd out the standalone questions the build actually needs.

### 4.3 What disconfirmation triggers
- **RQ-V weak / no standalone value** → the real reshape signal *for this build*: rethink what the module must do to stand on its own before committing. This — not H6 — is the near-term go/reshape lever.
- **H1–H5 absent/reshaped** → **rescope** the module (drop or re-aim a sub-competence); re-rank the sharpest pain.
- **H6a absent** → the daily-spiral motivation is weaker than assumed; lean the standalone value case on the other pains.
- **H6b absent → log to the integration backlog**; do not stop or reorder now. It becomes a live input to the pillar-ordering decision at integration (~9 months), when Reflect exists — not a Phase-1 gate. (Reconciled with canon via the decision log, 2026-07-23.)

## 5. Analysis approach

1. **Capture:** record + transcribe (consent permitting); otherwise structured notes with verbatim quotes for the key episodes.
2. **Code:** deductive coding against H1–H6 and the JTBD forces; inductive/open coding for emergent opportunities and unexpected pains.
3. **Rate per participant** using §4.1; keep the anchoring episode with each rating.
4. **Synthesise across participants:** cluster into the tree's **opportunity branch** (3–5 related opportunities, per canon Phase 1), each with a note on frequency, impact, and how currently-felt. Rank for RQ-P.
5. **Update the OST:** opportunities with reasoning; mark which are tested-solid vs. still Phase-1-fuzzy; record the explicit H6a/H6b read.
6. **Saturation check:** watch whether new interviews still surface new themes (see §6). Note what's still thin.

## 6. Sampling logic & sufficiency (summary; detail in Doc 3)

**The honest tension.** The engine's Phase 1 says *recruit 7–10, interview until 3–5 commit to test later*. The team currently has **three known candidate users** (English-speaking). Three is enough to *start* and to seed the eventual tester pool — but it is **below the level at which qualitative findings stabilise**. The most-cited evidence (Guest, Bunce & Johnson 2006) finds basic *meta-themes* appear by ~6 interviews and thematic saturation around ~12 in a homogeneous sample. At **n=3 we can generate and sharpen hypotheses, but we cannot confidently map the opportunity space or — critically — settle an architecture-level call on H6b.**

**Decision (2026-07-23): recruit toward 7–10.**
- Treat the three known users as the **seed** and the likely **committed prototype testers**, and **recruit additional participants toward the engine's 7–10** for the *discovery interviews* (screening in Doc 3).
- Hold **integration-bet (H6b) conclusions provisional** regardless — though this no longer gates the build (§2.4); **low-N remains the top validity threat** (§7) for the *standalone* findings and must be stated on the tree, not hidden.
- Apply variable depth: well-anchored H1–H5 can reach "good enough" faster; spend the freed time on RQ-V (standalone value) and the sharpest-pain read.

*(Exact number within the 7–10 target is set with Doc 3, against real availability.)*

## 7. Threats to validity & mitigations

| Threat | How it would fool us | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation bias** (we want the hypotheses true) | We fish, they oblige; "confirmed" is manufactured | Mom Test discipline; ask for stories not agreement; pre-commit the evidence signal; count *episodes*, not nods |
| **Courtesy / social-desirability bias** | Participants (esp. if they know _root) tell us what we want | Interviewer stays neutral; never reveal the hypothesis or the module; recruit beyond friends where possible |
| **Recall bias** | Reconstructed feelings drift | Anchor to a *specific recent* event; use the JTBD timeline to rebuild sequence |
| **Small-N over-reach** | We generalise from 3 | State N-limits on every finding; hold H6b provisional; recruit toward 7–10 |
| **Leading / founder-as-interviewer** | Our framing plants the answer | Open prompts; silence; "tell me more"; a second team member reviews transcripts for leading |
| **Construct invisibility** | People can't self-report the deficit | Observe in narration, don't ask them to grade (the core reason for interviews, §3.2) |
| **Language dilution** | English interviews under-elicit affect for Persian-dominant users | §9; re-probe key items with Persian-dominant users before locking H6b for the Persian product |

## 8. Ethics & participant wellbeing

These interviews ask people to revisit emotionally charged, sometimes painful episodes. That is not neutral, and the method must hold it with care (this also *is* the product's ethic — service and self-authorship, not extraction).

- **Distress protocol** (grounded in Whitney & Evered 2022): agree in advance what the interviewer does if a participant becomes distressed — pause, offer to stop, don't push; have a short list of support resources ready; make explicit that they can skip any question or end anytime.
- **Informed consent**: purpose, recording, storage, right to withdraw, and that this is research not therapy.
- **Boundaries**: we are not clinicians; do not probe trauma; stay at the level of everyday emotional episodes.
- **Data handling**: store transcripts privately; de-identify in shared notes; the sensitive-personal standard we already apply in this repo.
- **Interviewer care**: eliciting emotional material repeatedly has a cost; limit interviews per day and debrief between them.

## 9. Language & culture

Per canon (`../../canon/02-pillars/learn.md` §4) and the 22 Jul session: **Persian-first is the product constraint; this first discovery cohort is English-only** because the three known users are English-speaking. This is a legitimate build-order choice — but it carries a methodological caveat we must name:

> Affect labeling regulates only in a person's **dominant emotional language** (`../../canon/04-research/00-evidence-summary.md` §5). Interviewing about feelings in English with someone whose emotional language is Persian can **under-elicit** the very phenomena we're probing (esp. H1, H5, H6). So English-cohort findings on the *feeling-level* hypotheses generalise to the Persian product only tentatively.

**Plan:** run the English cohort now to sharpen the method and the module shape; **before locking H6b for the Persian product, re-run the key probes with Persian-dominant participants in Persian.** Flag this as an explicit gate, not an afterthought.

## 10. Definition of done for this research phase

Phase 1 (this research) is complete when we have:
1. **The module's standalone definition-of-done** — a crisp statement of what Module 1 must accomplish *on its own* to be worth using (from RQ-V), with evidence its mechanism delivers felt daily value independent of Reflect. *(Headline deliverable under the standalone-build model.)*
2. An **OST opportunity branch** — 3–5 related opportunities, each with reasoning and a frequency/impact/currently-felt note.
3. A **confirm / kill / reshape** verdict on each of H1–H5, each backed by real episodes.
4. A **ranked sharpest pain** (RQ-P) — the aim-point for the first solution.
5. An explicit read on **H6a** (standalone motivation) and **H6b** (integration bet) — with H6b's ordering implication **logged for the integration phase**, not treated as a Phase-1 gate.
6. A **validity/limits note** (N, language, biases) travelling with the findings.
7. Enough repetition that new interviews are mostly surfacing themes we've already seen (saturation-ish), *or* an honest statement of where we stopped short and why.

Deliverables 1–5 are what Phase 2 (generate solutions) picks up from the tree, with no meeting required (canon `../../canon/03-engine/02-async-coordination.md`).
