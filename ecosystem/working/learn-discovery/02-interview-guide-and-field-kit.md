# Learn Discovery · Doc 2 — Interview Guide & Field Kit

*The instrument for the Learn Module 1 discovery interviews: the guide, the question bank, and the prep/consent/coding materials. It implements the method chosen in Doc 1. This is a **guide, not a script** — follow the person's story; the research questions live in Doc 1 and stay in your head, never on the table.*

**Version 0.1 (draft) · 2026-07-23 · Owner: _root · Status: for review**
**Derives from:** `01-research-criteria-and-method.md` (method, RQs, ethics, language). **Upstream:** `../Learn feels and needs discovery.md` (hypotheses). **Participants/screening:** Doc 3 (next).
**Format:** one-to-one, semi-structured, ~50–60 min, **English** for this cohort. No questionnaire (Doc 1 §3.2; decision 2026-07-23: no scale).

---

## 1. The one thing to remember

We are **not** asking people to rate themselves or to like an idea. We are getting them to **tell real stories from their actual past**, and we are **listening for the phenomena** (the vague feeling, the "I felt ignored," the single-strategy fixation, the spiral) in how they narrate — because most of these gaps are invisible to the person who has them (Doc 1 §3.2). *Observe, don't quiz.* If you find yourself explaining a distinction or asking "do you struggle with X," stop and get another story.

**We never mention the module, the pillars, Reflect, "graduation," or that we're building anything.** The moment we pitch, the data is contaminated (Mom Test). If they ask "what's this for?": *"I'm in the early stages of something around emotional wellbeing and I'm just trying to understand real experiences first — I'd honestly rather not bias you by describing it yet. Can I show you later?"*

---

## 2. The golden rules (Mom Test discipline — hold these the whole time)

- **Talk about their life, not our idea.** Never describe a solution.
- **Past and specific, not general or future.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "do you usually…"; both beat "would you…". Anchor every claim to a real, datable event.
- **Dig into anchors, not opinions.** "That's great" is worthless; "last Tuesday I cried in the car because…" is gold.
- **Shut up.** Aim for them talking ~80%. Silence is a tool — count to three before filling it.
- **Follow emotion, not your list.** When they get animated or go quiet, that's the vein — mine it before moving on.
- **Don't lead.** No "so that must have been frustrating?" → instead "what was that like?"
- **Get concrete before you get abstract.** Story first, meaning second.

---

## 3. The techniques we're using (and why they're grounded)

| Technique | What it does here | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| **Story-based / Critical Incident Technique** | Ask for specific recent incidents ("the last time…"); give them a beat to recall; probe the incident, not the generality. Incidents are where pains show up in context. | Flanagan (1954), CIT; Torres story-based interviewing |
| **JTBD switch timeline + forces** | Reconstruct the sequence of the last time they sought to understand a feeling (first thought → trigger → what they tried → outcome) and read push / pull / anxiety / habit. Separates *real, live* pain from real-but-inert. | Moesta, forces of progress |
| **Laddering (means-end)** | For need-vs-strategy (H3): keep asking "if that happened, what would it give you?" to climb from a demand on one person to the underlying need. | Reynolds & Gutman, means-end/laddering |
| **Observe-don't-ask** | For the invisible-deficit hypotheses (H1, H2, H4, H5), elicit natural narration and code the phenomenon from it, rather than asking the person to self-assess the thing they can't see. | Doc 1 §3.2 (validity argument) |

## 4. The interview arc (with rough timings)

### 4.0 Pre-flight (before they arrive) — checklist in §9
Recording ready · consent to hand/read · distress card open (§7) · coding sheet open (§6) · quiet, private space · phone silenced · water available.

### 4.1 Open & consent — ~5 min
Read the **consent script** (§7.1). Get explicit OK to record. Set the frame: *no right answers, nothing to sell, skip/stop anytime.*

### 4.2 Warm-up / grand tour — ~5 min
Low-stakes, gets them into storytelling mode:
- "Before anything heavy — tell me a bit about what your last week or two has been like."
- "Walk me through a normal day for you lately."
Listen for a natural thread to follow; note any emotional episodes to return to.

### 4.3 The switch story (JTBD core) — ~12–15 min
The spine of the interview. Open wide, then rebuild the timeline:
- **Main prompt:** *"Tell me about the last time you were going through something emotionally — something that was sitting with you — and you tried to make sense of what you were feeling. Take me back to it."*
- **Timeline follow-ups:** "When did you first notice something was up?" → "What was going on around then?" → "What did you actually do?" → "Then what?" → "How did it end up?"
- **Forces:** "What pushed you to *do* something about it rather than just let it ride?" (push) · "What were you hoping would happen?" (pull) · "Was there anything that made you hesitate?" (anxiety) · "What would you normally do instead?" (habit).
Most of H1–H6 will surface *inside this story*. Mine it before reaching for §5.

### 4.4 Hypothesis deep-probes — ~15–20 min
Using material they've *already* given, deepen with the threads in **§5**. Don't run all of them — run the ones their story opened, and always reserve time for **H6a/H6b** (a reflection/journaling episode) and **RQ-V** (did clarity ever pay off).

### 4.5 What they've tried & dropped — ~5 min
- "What have you tried for this kind of thing — apps, journaling, talking to someone, therapy, anything?"
- "What worked, even a little?" · "What did you stop using — and what made you stop?"
Grounds the alternatives landscape and the abandonment pattern (personal-informatics: people quit mostly from lost motivation, not broken features).

### 4.6 Close — ~5 min
- "What should I have asked you that I didn't?"
- "Anything you want to add?"
- Thank them sincerely.
- *(Optional, only if they fit the tester pool — Doc 3):* "Down the line I might have a rough thing to try — no obligation — could I come back to you?" Keep it a soft, separate ask; never a pitch.

## 5. The question bank — per hypothesis (ask this, not that, listen for)

*Use as raw material, not a checklist. Every "ask this" is Mom-Test-safe (past, specific, non-leading). Every "listen for" is what you code in §6.*

### RQ-A / RQ-B — is there a real job, and is the pain live?
- **Ask:** the switch-story prompt (§4.3) + the four forces.
- **Not:** "Would you use something that helps you understand your emotions?" · "Do you wish you were better with feelings?"
- **Listen for:** whether a real trigger ever pushed them to act; what they hired; whether the pain was strong enough to move them or just background.

### H1 — coarse emotional resolution
- **Ask:** "You said you felt [off / bad / weird] — take me back into that moment. What was it actually like in you?" · "Did you have a word for it at the time, or not really?"
- **Not:** "Do you find it hard to name your emotions?" (self-assess)
- **Listen for:** genuine vagueness ("just bad," "I don't know, off"); whether the not-knowing itself bothered them or stalled them from acting.

### H2 — feelings confused with judgements of others *(observe; don't correct)*
- **Ask:** in their conflict story — "When you felt [ignored / dismissed / disrespected], what had *just happened*? What did you do next?"
- **Not:** teaching them "that's a thought, not a feeling," or asking "do you confuse thoughts and feelings?"
- **Listen for:** faux-feelings ("ignored, let down, abandoned, manipulated") narrated *as feelings*; blame framing; a powerless/stuck ending.

### H3 — needs confused with strategies *(ladder up)*
- **Ask:** "You wanted them to [text back / show up / say sorry] — if they *had*, what would that have given you?" → then "and what would *that* give you?" (keep climbing) · "Was there any other way you might have gotten that?"
- **Not:** "Do you know the difference between a need and a strategy?"
- **Listen for:** fixation on one person/one route; whether they can *see* any alternative path to the same underlying need; strain/stuckness when the one route failed.

### H4 — emotions felt as fixed & external
- **Ask:** "In that moment, did it feel like there was anything you could do about how you felt — or was it just going to run its course?" · "Have you ever shifted a feeling on purpose? What did you do?"
- **Not:** "Do you believe emotions are changeable?" (abstract belief quiz)
- **Listen for:** "that's just how I am," "it takes over," at-the-mercy language vs. any sense of agency/deliberate influence.

### H5 — disconnection from bodily signal
- **Ask:** "When you felt that — where did you notice it in your body? What was the texture — tight, heavy, buzzy, hot?"
- **Not:** "Are you in touch with your body?"
- **Listen for:** can they locate/describe a sensation (tractable) or do they go blank / jump straight to the story / find the question strange.

### H6a — in-head reflection spirals *(standalone-relevant)*
- **Ask:** "When something's bothering you and you turn it over in your head, does it tend to *settle*, or go in circles? Tell me about a recent time."
- **Listen for:** looping/rumination vs. reaching some resolution; how it ended.

### H6b — reconstrual is the lever *(integration-relevant, deferred — still probe)*
- **Ask:** for a journaling / talked-it-out instance — "Did writing it down / talking it out *change how you saw* the situation, or mostly repeat what you already felt?"
- **Listen for:** a genuine reframe / new angle (reconstrual) vs. venting/recounting; whether the times it *helped* were the times they saw it differently.

### RQ-V — does clarity pay off standalone? *(the primary question — let it emerge)*
- **Ask:** "Was there a time when getting *clear* on what you were feeling actually changed what you did next, or how it went? Tell me about it." · "When you finally landed on the right word for something — did anything shift?"
- **Listen for:** a concrete real-life payoff of emotional clarity — a decision made, a conversation handled, a de-escalation, relief, an action taken. This is the evidence that the capability is worth having *on its own*, with no Reflect attached.

### RQ-G — does naming ever make it worse? *(the anti-goal guardrail)*
- **Ask:** "Has trying to sit with or dig into a feeling ever left you feeling *worse*? What happened?"
- **Listen for:** naming/analysis amplifying distress (the notice→analyse→spiral failure mode); and whether *doing something small* helped it move.

*(RQ-P — sharpest pain — isn't a question. After each interview, note in §6 which pain was most vivid/frequent/impactful **for this person**; the ranking is a cross-interview synthesis.)*

## 6. Observation & coding sheet (fill during + immediately after)

*One per participant. Capture the **anchoring episode/quote**, not just the verdict — the tree carries reasoning, not conclusions.*

```
  Push / Pull / Anxiety / Habit: ____________________________________
  What they hired / tried: __________  What they dropped & why: ______

HYPOTHESIS VERDICTS   (Confirmed / Absent / Reshaped + episode-quote)
  H1 coarse resolution ......... [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H2 feelings-as-judgements .... [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H3 need-vs-strategy .......... [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H4 entity stance ............. [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H5 body disconnection ........ [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H6a in-head spiral ........... [ ]  " ________________________ "
  H6b reconstrual-is-lever ..... [ ]  " ________________________ "

RQ-V  clarity paid off standalone?  [Y/N/partial]  episode: __________
RQ-G  naming ever made it worse?    [Y/N]          episode: __________

SHARPEST PAIN for THIS person (RQ-P input): ________________________
EMERGENT (a pain/opportunity we didn't predict): __________________
VALIDITY FLAGS (leading? courtesy? recall? English-not-their-first-language dilution?): ____
```

Rating rule (Doc 1 §4.1): **Confirmed** = a specific real episode shows the pain, unprompted; **Absent** = asked well, no such episode; **Reshaped** = a related-but-different pain surfaced (edit the tree — a signal, not a failure).

## 7. Ethics, consent & distress (keep this card visible during the interview)

These interviews touch real emotional pain. Holding that well *is the product's ethic* (service, not extraction), not just compliance. Grounded in the qualitative distress-protocol literature (Whitney & Evered, 2022).

### 7.1 Consent script (read/paraphrase warmly)
> "Thanks for making the time. I'm trying to understand how people notice and make sense of their emotions in everyday life — the real, messy version. A few things first: there are no right or wrong answers, and nothing you say is judged. I'm not going to show you or sell you anything — I just want your experience. Some of this might touch personal or hard moments — you can skip anything, take a break, or stop completely at any point, no reason needed. Is it OK if I record, just so I can actually listen instead of scribbling? It stays private to our small team and I keep it de-identified. Good to start?"

### 7.2 Distress protocol (if they become upset)
- **Slow down, soften.** "We can pause right here — there's no need to push into this."
- **Ask, don't assume:** "Do you want to keep going, take a break, or stop?" — and honour it.
- **Don't counsel or interpret.** You are not their therapist. Hold space; don't fix, diagnose, or reframe.
- **If real distress:** leave the topic, return to something lighter, close gently. Encourage them to lean on someone they trust or a professional; keep a local support/crisis contact on hand.
- **Before ending:** check they're OK. Thank them sincerely.

### 7.3 Boundaries & data
- Stay at the level of **everyday emotional episodes**; do **not** probe trauma. If someone goes there, gently steer back.
- Store recordings/transcripts **privately**; de-identify in any shared notes (the sensitive-personal standard we already apply in this repo).

### 7.4 Interviewer care
Eliciting emotional material repeatedly has a cost (vicarious load). **Max ~2–3 interviews/day**, debrief with your observer after each, don't stack them back-to-back.

## 8. Immediately-after debrief (10 min, while it's fresh — do not skip)
With the observer, before the next interview or the day's end:
1. Fill/finish the §6 sheet from memory + recording.
2. One line: **what surprised you?**
3. **Did I lead anywhere?** Flag any question that may have planted an answer (for the next interview).
4. **New probe to add / drop** for the next round.
5. Are we still hearing new themes, or repeats? (saturation check — Doc 1 §5).

## 9. Prep & logistics
- **Pairing (recommended, per onboarding plan):** one **interviewer** (carries the conversation), one **observer/coder** (silent, runs the §6 sheet, watches for leading). Reduces the founder-as-sole-interviewer bias (Doc 1 §7).
- **Pre-flight checklist:** recording tool tested · consent ready · distress card (§7) open · coding sheet (§6) open · quiet private space · notifications off · water.
- **Remote vs. in person:** either works; if remote, ensure a private space on their end and that recording consent is explicit on the recording. *(Final logistics decided with Doc 3.)*
- **One at a time.** Don't batch beyond the §7.4 limit.
- **Thank-you / incentive:** decided with Doc 3.

## 10. Adaptations & limits
- **English now, Persian later.** This kit is English for the current cohort. Because affect-labeling regulates only in one's dominant language (Doc 1 §9), the *feeling-level* probes (H1, H5, and the "find the word" moments) may under-elicit for Persian-dominant participants. **Before locking H6b for the Persian product, re-run the H1/H5/H6 threads in Persian with Persian-dominant participants** — a translated kit, not a literal translation (idioms for feelings/needs differ).
- **Don't over-run H6b.** It's the deferred integration bet (Doc 1 §2.4); give RQ-V and the sharpest-pain read the primary time.
