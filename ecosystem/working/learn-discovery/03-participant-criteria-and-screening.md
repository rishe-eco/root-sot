## 1. What this decides
The interviews are only as good as who's in the room. This doc sets **inclusion/exclusion criteria**, the **sampling strategy**, a ready-to-use **screener**, the **numbers**, and the **recruitment approach** — all tuned to the one constraint that matters most here: we need people who *have the pains* (so there's something real to observe) **without** pre-selecting only people who'd love our solution (which would manufacture confirmation). See Doc 1 §7 on why that bias is the main threat.

---

## 2. Sampling strategy (grounded)

**Purposive, not random** — the standard for discovery. Specifically a blend (Patton's typology):

- **Criterion sampling** — everyone must meet the core must-haves in §3. This is the floor.
- **Light maximum-variation** — deliberately spread across a few axes (§4) so we can see which pains **cut across** different kinds of people (a pain that shows up in reflectors *and* non-reflectors is a stronger opportunity than one that only appears in journalers).
- …within a **broadly homogeneous frame** (adults, English-speaking, everyday-emotional-episode experience, not in crisis) so that saturation is meaningful — Guest et al.'s ~6-for-metathemes / ~12-for-saturation evidence assumes a homogeneous sample (Doc 1 §6).
- **Snowball / chain** for reach — extend from the known users' networks *one step removed* (friends-of-friends, not close friends — see §5 on courtesy bias).

The logic: criterion sampling guarantees relevance; max-variation guards generalisation; homogeneity keeps saturation interpretable; snowball makes it feasible for a three-person team.

## 3. Who we're looking for — core inclusion criteria (must-haves)

A participant qualifies only if **all** hold:

1. **Adult** (18+).
2. **Comfortable being interviewed in English** for ~1 hour, and OK with a **private, de-identified recording**.
3. **Has live material** — in the recent past (last month or two) they had an emotionally significant episode they *tried, or wanted, to make sense of*. This is the "has the problem" gate, screened **behaviourally** (a concrete instance), never by self-label ("I'm an emotional person"). Recruit for behaviour, not demographics.
4. **Willing to talk** about personal emotional experiences with a stranger/acquaintance.
5. **Emotionally steady enough** to revisit past difficult moments safely — **not currently in acute crisis, recent trauma/bereavement, or a fragile state** (ethical gate — §5, §9).

Note we are **not** screening for our target *brand* audience (values-defined, self-authorship-minded). At discovery we want people with the *pains*, across types — the audience question is separate.

---

## 4. Variation axes — spread the sample across these

Aim for a real spread on each; the point is to test whether the H1–H6 pains generalise.

| Axis | Why it matters | Aim for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflection habit** (journal / talk-it-out / think-it-through vs. distract / avoid) | H6a/H6b are best seen by **contrasting** people for whom reflection *helps* vs. *spirals*; H1/H5 show sharpest in non-reflectors | a real mix of reflectors and non-reflectors — **don't let it skew to journalers** |
| **Prior tools** (therapy/coaching/apps/books vs. none) | tests whether pains persist even for the "already-worked-on-it" crowd; grounds the alternatives/abandonment picture | some with, some without |
| **Emotional articulacy** (fluent vs. "fine / stressed / bad") | H1 (coarse resolution) is most visible in the low-articulacy end | include several low-articulacy people, not only the self-aware |
| **Life-context spread** (gender, rough age band, situation) | avoids a monoculture; cheap variation | a modest spread, not a quota |

## 5. Exclusion / cap criteria (and why)

- **Exclude — acute crisis or fragility.** Anyone currently in severe distress, recent trauma/bereavement, or an unstable mental-health period. This is an **ethical** exclusion (we're doing formative product research, not clinical work, and must not risk harm — §9), and it also confounds. Handled gently at screen (§8 Q6) and backed by the distress protocol (Doc 2 §7).
- **Exclude / cap at ≤1 — trained insiders.** People with formal training in **NVC, emotion-focused therapy, counselling, or heavy contemplative practice** already have the meta-language the module teaches; they'll narrate cleanly in feelings/needs terms and **mask the naive phenomena** we're trying to observe. At most one, as a deliberate contrast — flagged as such.
- **Cap — close friends & family of the team.** The three known users and any close contacts carry **courtesy/social-desirability bias** (they want us to succeed). Use them as seed, but **weight the sample toward people one step removed** who aren't invested in our success (Mom Test / Torres). Track referral source (§8 Q7, §11).
- **Screen out — gamers / "professional participants."** Low risk here (not a paid panel), but keep the screener **non-revealing** so no one can tailor answers to qualify (§8).

## 6. Numbers, and the interview-vs-tester distinction

**Target (decided 2026-07-23): recruit toward 7–10 interviews.**
- **Recruit ~10–12 qualified** to actually *run ~8–10* (buffer for no-shows and drop-outs).
- Maps to the evidence: ~6 gets meta-themes, ~12 approaches saturation in a homogeneous sample — 7–10 is the pragmatic sweet spot for a small-team discovery: enough to see patterns repeat and sharpen the module, **not** enough to settle the architecture-level H6b call (which is deferred anyway — Doc 1 §2.4). State the N-limit on every finding.

**Interview pool ≠ tester pool.** Per the engine's Phase 1: from the interviews, secure **3–5 who agree to test a prototype later**. The three known users are the likeliest testers. Keep the "would you try a rough version down the line?" ask **soft and separate** from the interview (Doc 2 §4.6) — never a pitch.

**The three known users** are the **seed**: interview them (they have live material and are willing), *and* use them to snowball outward. Don't let the sample *be* them plus their inner circle.

---

## 7. Recruitment approach & channels

- **Snowball from the seed, one step removed.** Ask the three known users (and the team) for intros to people who fit — *not* their closest friends. This reaches real people fast while diluting courtesy bias.
- **Relevant communities, carefully.** Self-development / journaling / student groups can work, but they **over-select reflective people** — if used, deliberately balance with non-reflectors (§4). Don't let the channel choose the sample.
- **Keep intent hidden.** Outreach describes the *topic*, not the hypotheses or any product.

**Sample outreach blurb (neutral, non-revealing):**
> "I'm doing some informal research on how people experience and make sense of their emotions in everyday life — just listening to real experiences, nothing to sell or test. It's a relaxed ~45–60 min chat, private and recorded only so I can listen properly, and you can skip anything or stop anytime. Would you be up for it, or know someone who might?"

## 8. The screener (ready to use)

A short form or 5-minute pre-call. **Funnel order: eligibility/elimination first, least-revealing questions before specific ones, behaviour over demographics, intent hidden.**

1. **Eligibility (elimination):** 18+? · Comfortable with a ~1-hour chat **in English**? · OK to be **recorded** (private, de-identified)? — *[any "no" → not eligible]*
2. **Live material (behavioural gate — non-revealing):** "In the past month or two, was there a time you felt something strongly — good or hard — and found yourself trying to make sense of it? Roughly what was it about?" — *[a concrete instance → qualifies; "can't think of one" → likely no live material, deprioritise]*
3. **Reflection habit (variation):** "When something's on your mind, what do you usually do with it — talk to someone, write it down, think it over, distract yourself, something else?"
4. **Prior tools (variation + insider flag):** "Have you ever used anything for this kind of thing — journaling, an app, therapy or coaching, a book, a course? Which?" — *[flags NVC/therapy training → §5 cap]*
5. **(Coder note, not a question):** how granular is their language in Q2/Q3? (articulacy axis)
6. **Safety self-check (gentle, non-clinical):** "The chat would touch on everyday emotional experiences, sometimes ones that were difficult — nothing clinical, and you can skip or stop anytime. Is now an okay time in your life for a conversation like that?" — *[self-selection out; if any doubt, don't proceed — §9]*
7. **Referral source (bias tracking):** "How did we get connected?" — *[close friend/family of team → cap]*
8. **Logistics:** availability; remote or in person; best contact.

Do **not** mention emotional-literacy, feelings-vs-needs, "the module," or that we're building anything. Leading screeners let people game qualification and reveal intent.

---

## 9. Safety & ethics in screening

- The crisis exclusion (§5, §8 Q6) is done **gently and non-clinically** — we are not qualified to assess mental health and won't try. It's a **self-selection** invitation plus interviewer judgement, backed by the in-interview **distress protocol** (Doc 2 §7).
- Make the **voluntary, stop-anytime** nature clear at screen *and* at consent.
- If someone discloses they're in a hard place, **don't recruit them for this**; respond like a decent human, point them to support if appropriate, and thank them.
- Privacy: hold contact details and screener answers to the same **sensitive-personal** standard as the rest of this repo.

## 10. Selecting the final set (balancing)

From qualified candidates, pick the ~8–10 to actually interview so the set **spans the §4 axes** — in particular:
- **not more than ~half habitual reflectors** (protect H1/H5/H6 contrast);
- **a mix** of therapy/tools-experienced and not;
- **several low-articulacy** people, not only the self-aware;
- **not all from one referral source**, and **not dominated by team-close contacts**;
- **≤1 trained insider**, flagged.

If the pool skews (e.g., all reflective journalers volunteered), **name the skew as a limitation** and try one more channel before concluding — don't quietly let the sample bias the findings.

## 12. The Persian cohort (later — flagged, not now)
This cohort is English (Doc 1 §9). Before locking **H6b** and the feeling-level findings for the **Persian product**, run a **separate Persian-dominant cohort** — its own criteria and a **translated (not literal) screener + guide**, since feelings/needs idioms differ. Same sampling logic; new participants. This is an explicit gate, not an afterthought.

## 13. Open items / decisions
- **Exact N** within 7–10, and **how many testers** to lock (target 3–5) — against real availability.
- **Incentive / thank-you** — a genuine thanks; decide if anything more (kept modest to avoid attracting "professional participants").
- **Who owns recruitment & scheduling**, and **who interviews vs. observes** (Doc 2 §9).
- **Channel mix** — how far to lean on communities vs. pure snowball, given the over-reflective-skew risk.
- **Dry-run participant** — the one friendly non-participant for the guide pilot (Doc 2 §11) sits *outside* this sample.
