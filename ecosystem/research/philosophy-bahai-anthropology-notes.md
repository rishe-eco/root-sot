## What was asked
Where does modern psychology's concept of *mental health* sit within ʻAbdu'l-Bahá's anthropological model in *Some Answered Questions* (Mofāvazāt)? Three sub-questions: (1) what "mental health" means in modern psychology and its underlying mindset; (2) how ʻAbdu'l-Bahá's model is structured; (3) where mental health locates within it.

## Analysis produced
- Modern psychology's **negative** (absence of disorder) and **positive** (flourishing) definitions; its methodological naturalism and the normativity it smuggles in.
- A **three-register decomposition** of "mental health" within the model:
  1. **neurophysiological instrument integrity** (the body/brain as instrument),
  2. **right ordering of the inner faculties** governed by reason,
  3. **spiritual orientation**, understood through *privation* (deficiency rather than a substance).
- The **"replaceable scaffolding" question**: does the faculty framework carry revealed weight, or is it revisable scientific vocabulary? — flagged as the direction to pursue next.

## Text-verification pass (self-corrections banked)
A multi-chapter review against the Persian original alongside the 2014 authorized English (Ocean of Lights + Bahá'í Reference Library) produced two corrections:
- The **estimative faculty (wahm) is NOT in ʻAbdu'l-Bahá's five inner powers.** The list is **imagination, thought, comprehension, memory, and the common faculty** — already a departure from Avicenna's canonical scheme (i.e., don't attribute the Avicennian *wahm* to the text).
- The **"broken lute / madness"** passage often cited is **not in the Mofāvazāt** — do not attribute it to that book.

## Enrichments from the review
- The **bidirectional human spirit** (capable of both greatest perfection and greatest deficiency) — from the *Five Kinds of Spirit* chapter.
- An explicit **psychosomatic causal channel** in the healing chapters; a stated **ceiling on spiritual healing** overriding material illness.
- The **two-axis subtle/palpable torment** distinction (*Two Kinds of Torment*).
- The **Four Criteria** chapter read as an **internal warrant** for treating the model's own scaffolding as fallible and revisable — which is what keeps this compatible with Root's "reality over fiction" and evidence-grading stance.
