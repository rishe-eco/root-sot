That’s an excellent question — and it shows you’re thinking like a builder, not just a planner.

You’ve nailed the problem most self-taught entrepreneurs face: turning conceptual understanding (business models, marketing, management) into quantitative, evidence-based strategy — the kind investors, mentors, and markets take seriously.

Let’s break it down clearly.

🧭 Your Core Goal

You want to translate your business idea into a data-backed model — one that shows:

Who your customers really are, and how many exist.

How your competitors perform and position themselves.

What the financial viability (pricing, costs, margins, growth) looks like.

This means developing literacy in market analysis, competitive analysis, and financial modeling.

1️⃣ Start with the Right Umbrella Field

There isn’t one course that teaches it all — but there is one field that combines these elements:

→ Market Research & Business Analytics

This is the bridge between “theory of business” and “real-world numbers.”

Studying (even self-studying) market research will teach you:

How to define and size a market (TAM, SAM, SOM).

How to collect and interpret customer data (surveys, interviews, analytics).

How to benchmark competitors quantitatively.

How to segment customers based on data, not assumptions.

And pairing it with basic business analytics will help you:

Use data (from public sources or your own experiments) to make forecasts.

Create pricing, cost, and revenue models in spreadsheets.

Build a bottom-up financial model (expected growth, margins, runway).

2️⃣ Step-by-Step Learning Path

Here’s a practical roadmap you can follow:

Step 1: Market & Competitor Research Basics

Learn:

Market sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM framework).

Customer segmentation (demographics, psychographics, behavioral).

Industry analysis (Porter’s Five Forces, SWOT with data).

Competitive analysis (how to find and analyze competitors quantitatively).

Resources:

Philip Kotler’s “Marketing Management” (solid grounding).

Coursera/edX courses like “Market Research and Consumer Behavior” (IE Business School, free).

Look up “How to conduct a competitive analysis” by HubSpot or CB Insights.

Step 2: Data & Analytical Thinking

Learn how to work with numbers in simple tools (Google Sheets or Excel):

How to build simple financial models.

How to calculate and interpret metrics: CAC, LTV, churn, gross margin.

How to forecast based on assumptions and real data.

Resources:

YouTube channels like Startup Finance and CFO Perspective.

Book: Financial Intelligence for Entrepreneurs by Karen Berman & Joe Knight.

Course: “Business Analytics Specialization” (Wharton on Coursera).

Step 3: Real-World Practice

Pick a few competitors and practice:

Quantifying their market share, pricing, and positioning.

Estimating customer segments from public data (reviews, social media, etc.).

Modeling what your numbers could look like with real market assumptions.

Tools to use:

Google Trends, SimilarWeb, SEMrush, Statista (for data).

LinkedIn + Crunchbase (for competitor & market discovery).

Excel or Notion for your own model.

Step 4: Financial Modeling

Finally, tie it all together with a financial model for your business:

Revenue model (how you make money).

Cost structure.

Unit economics.

Growth and cash flow projections.

Courses:

“Financial Modeling for Startups” (Udemy / CFI).

Y Combinator’s Startup School (free and practical).

3️⃣ Recommended Learning Sequence (Summary)
Stage	What to Learn	Focus Tools/Frameworks
1	Market Research	TAM/SAM/SOM, segmentation
2	Competitor Analysis	SWOT, 5 Forces, benchmarking
3	Business Analytics	Excel modeling, KPI tracking
4	Financial Modeling	Unit economics, cash flow, runway
5	Integration	Business plan w/ real numbers
🧩 Tip

If you want a single “bridge” skill that will make everything else easier, learn how to use data to tell business stories — that’s the superpower behind successful founders and analysts alike.
