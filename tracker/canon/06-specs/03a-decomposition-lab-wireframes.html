Decomposition Lab — wireframes · draft 1

Low-fidelity. Companion to 03-decomposition-lab.md §10. Ten plates plus an RTL pass. Two components are genuinely new (5 the breakdown canvas, 6 the recomposition reveal); the rest rearranges what Clarity and Evidence Lab already have. Each frame is tagged to the rubric criterion or the design rule it protects — the guardrails are the design. Worked scenario throughout: move a two-person household to another city in five weeks.

1Tool home — and what a first visit must not show

Evidence Lab learned this from real use: a first visit that opens on zeroed metrics presents eight unanswered questions in place of an explanation. Statistics appear only once there is progress.

2The costume, named once — d0

Unscored, one screen, re-openable. The only place the tool discusses prompting technique at all.

3Concept, then a labelled contrast pair

Unlabelled examples are the weak intervention (d ≈ 0.25). The subgoal labels are what carry the effect — so the pair always ships with them, and the failure is named on the frame rather than left to be inferred.

4The whole, stated and locked

D1 is unscoreable if pieces can precede it, and the ordering is the measurement — so the statement is stamped server-side and is not editable once the first piece exists. Same category as Clarity's prediction lock and Evidence's verdict lock.

5The breakdown canvas — new component

The one place the design will fight normal editor instincts. No suggestion affordance of any kind — no "generate a breakdown", no autocomplete, no ghost text. Decomposition is the most offloadable skill in the stack: a model produces a plausible breakdown instantly, and an assist button here would train the offloading rather than the skill. Order of authoring is logged throughout, because that is how D2 is scored.

6The recomposition reveal — new component

The direct analogue of Clarity's gap reveal, and the mechanic the tool turns on: the learner commits, then the pieces are played back against the whole they themselves stated. In the closest published study, 51 of 55 student breakdowns were missing required functionality and two specific elements were absent from about 70% — so the missing piece is the normal case, not the punishment case.

7Feedback — six criteria, and an unscored one that says so

Per-criterion, each justified by the learner's own piece. Aggregate last, not first. Two display rules are pinned by test rather than left to styling.

8The control item — where the answer is "leave it alone"

About a third of all items. Evidence Lab's refusal, transposed: a learner who breaks everything into twenty pieces has not learned decomposition, they have acquired a different failure. Splitting something atomic must cost exactly what missing a piece costs.

9Real work — the one screen that can damage real data

The learner decomposes an actual Goal, and may push the result into real milestones and projects. Item by item, reviewed, reversible. Never automatic — an auto-write into somebody's real tree would be unforgivable, and this is the plate to get wrong quietly.

10RTL pass — fa

Persian ships with the surface, not after it. This tool is the cheapest of the three to bilingualise — required-element sets, atomic markers, overlap pairs and blocking graphs are all locale-invariant, and unlike Clarity's R4/R6 there is no Persian-specific linguistic work. What does need a pass is direction: the tree indents from the right, sequence badges lead, dependency chips mirror.

What these plates are protecting
  • No suggestion affordance anywhere in the canvas (plate 5). Decomposition is the most offloadable skill in the stack; every convenience proposed here converts the exercise into the failure it exists to prevent.
  • The whole is stated and locked before any piece exists (plate 4). D1 is unscoreable otherwise, and coverage has nothing to be measured against.
  • Three relations, three visually distinct notations (plate 5) — nesting, sequence badge, dashed dependency chip. Published diagrams blend hierarchy and sequence and then become unscoreable; a single arrow type reproduces that and blames the learner.
  • Commit before reveal (plate 6). The recomposition gap is shown only after BREAKDOWN_LOCKED, and the gap is quoted from the learner's own done condition.
  • Unscored is not zero (plate 7). The words "not scored", and a total that reads 9 / 10 · 5 of 6 criteria — never a silent 9 / 12.
  • Over-decomposition is a scored error (plate 8). ~⅓ control items where the correct answer is one piece; the headline is discrimination, not coverage.
  • The real-data write is itemised, opt-in and reversible (plate 9), and it states what it drops.
  • Statistics only once there is progress (plate 1), and Persian ships with the surface (plate 10).

Settled 2026-08-11, after review. (a) Enforced level structure, not a free canvas — the levels are part of the teaching, and D2 is unmeasurable without a real notion of "this level". (b) The d4 badge appears live but reports absence only: "no done condition yet", never a green affirmation. Live feedback is worth having and the gaming risk was the green badge, not the amber one — you cannot farm a level by clearing a warning, and D4's top level additionally requires not having split anything the key marks atomic. (c) The d5 honesty note appears on the export screen as well as in-module, because the export is where dependency information actually dies.
