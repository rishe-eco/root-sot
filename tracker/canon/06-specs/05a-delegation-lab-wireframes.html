Delegation Lab — wireframes · draft 1

Low-fidelity. Companion to 05-delegation-lab.md §10. Ten plates plus an RTL pass. The new component is the three-position reveal (plate 4) — your estimate, the advice, the truth, and the distance you actually moved. Everything else rearranges the three existing labs. Two worked items run throughout: a straight-line distance where the advice deserves weight, and a historical date where it does not.

1Tool home — two numbers that are never added together

Over-reliance and under-reliance sit side by side in a single bordered pair, and there is no view anywhere that sums them. The published warning about this skill is exact: interventions reduce one failure by increasing the other, and a combined “reliance” figure would render that trade as progress.

2Estimate first — and the advice does not exist yet

Weight of advice is meaningless if the initial estimate can move after the advice is seen, and it is the one number the entire tool rests on. So the estimate and the confidence are committed together, server-stamped, and the advice is absent from the payload until they are — not hidden, not disabled.

3Which cue did you use?

The costume made selectable. The list mixes category claims about AI in general with instance-level cues about this task — and on items where nothing legitimately discriminates, the correct answer is to say so. Enumerated so it stays keyed, offline and comparable across locales.

4The three-position reveal — new component

Your estimate, the advice, the truth, and the distance you actually travelled between them. Two frames because the two failures are different lessons and must never be collapsed into “your weighting was off” — one learner ignored help, the other took harm.

5Stakes — the same question, scored on the difference

A pair is one unit. Neither half is scored alone, because the measure is whether reliance moved with the cost of being wrong. The correct answer to the same question is not the same when the downside changes.

6The advisor sequence — three rounds, one visible error

The only multi-item unit in the engine, and the only way to see algorithm aversion happen. Round 1 establishes the learner's weighting, the advisor is visibly wrong on round 2, round 3 measures the response. One attempt containing three rounds, never three attempts. Three rather than five (D-24): algorithm aversion is the drop, and one post-error observation measures a drop — whether a learner who overshoots then recovers is a plausible pattern that is not in the literature, so it is a deferred question rather than a doubled sitting.

7Real work — the only mode that spans two sittings

Logged before the decision and revisited after, because a delegation judgement recorded afterwards is a memory of a judgement. This is the tool's durable artifact, the counterpart to Evidence's verdict card and Verification's record.

8RTL pass — fa

The cheapest of the five tools to bilingualise: the instrument is numeric, so estimates, advice, truth and every derived metric are locale-invariant. Only scenario prose and cue labels are realised twice, and there is no Persian-specific linguistic work at all. Western digits in both locales.

What these plates are protecting
  • The advice does not exist client-side until the estimate is committed (plate 2). WOA is meaningless if the initial estimate can move afterwards, and it is the one number the whole tool rests on.
  • Confidence commits with the estimate (plate 2), never after the advice — otherwise it measures hindsight.
  • Over- and under-reliance are drawn as one bordered pair and never summed (plate 1). The literature's clearest warning is that interventions trade them; a combined figure would show that trade as progress.
  • Calibration and discrimination are shown as separate lines with no causal arrow between them (plate 1). The first reliably improves; whether it moves the second is exactly the open question, and the learner sees it unresolved.
  • The two failures get different copy (plate 4) — you ignored help and you took harm are not the same lesson.
  • WOA never appears during an item, only at the reveal. Shown live, the item becomes an exercise in hitting a target.
  • Advice is plausible-wrong, never broken-wrong (plate 4). Advice you would dismiss on sight teaches nothing.
  • The capability list is shown, dated and struck through (plate 3), rather than warned against.
  • A stakes pair is one unit (plate 5) and a sequence is one attempt of three rounds (plate 6).
  • The number line does not mirror in RTL (plate 8).

Settled 2026-08-11, after review. (a) The stakes criterion accepts either lever. Expected cost of delegating is P(error) × cost(error), and a cheap check collapses the second term — so under high stakes, reducing reliance and keeping reliance while making it recoverable are both correct readings, and only treating the two framings identically is wrong. The recoverability move is recorded; whether the check is any good is Verification Lab's measurement and is never scored here. This keeps the concept and restores the precision: a learner weak at #4 does not lose points on a delegation criterion. (b) The population baseline is withheld until the baseline probe completes (or 12 scored items if it was skipped), then shown permanently on the progress surface only — never during an item, never at a reveal, since an anchor delivered early cannot be withdrawn. (c) Three rounds, behind a G6_ROUNDS constant — D-24.
