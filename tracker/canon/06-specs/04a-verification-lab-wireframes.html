Verification Lab — wireframes · draft 1

Low-fidelity. Companion to 04-verification-lab.md §10, with the two rungs from §4a. Ten plates plus an RTL pass. Two components are new — the oracle bench (3, 4) and the ritual-first reveal (7); the rest rearranges what the three existing labs already have. Worked item throughout: an AI answer about winter heating costs that is confidently, arithmetically wrong.

1Tool home — rungs are per module

The first-visit rule is unchanged from the other labs and not redrawn here: no statistics until there is progress. What is new is the rung marker on each module, and ritual rate sitting beside the composite rather than buried.

2Name the oracle before the bench opens

V1 is unscoreable if the checks can precede it, and the ordering is the measurement — so oracle_named is server-stamped and the bench is disabled until it exists. Fourth tool, fourth forcing function, same table.

3The oracle bench — new component, two rungs

Every check shows what it costs before you run it and nothing about what it returns. Spending is irreversible. That is the entire decision the learner has to make, and a bench that previewed outcomes would measure nothing. The two rungs differ only in whether the spend runs against a ceiling.

4The costumes are on the bench on purpose

“Ask it if it's sure” and “ask for a confidence score” are the two things most widely taught as verification. Both are cheap, both are satisfying, and both return the same thing whether or not the answer is wrong. A costume you are prevented from wearing is a costume you never learn to recognise — so they are selectable, priced low, and they pay out exactly nothing.

5Commit — everything together, before anything is revealed

Verdict, failing element, confidence and residual risk are committed as one act and are not editable afterwards. The enumerated element list is never on screen while checks are being chosen — earlier, it becomes a shopping guide for which check to buy, contaminating V1 and V3.

6The ritual-first reveal — new component

The most important plate in the set. If every check the learner ran was pass-either-way, the reveal says so before it says whether they were right — because a correct verdict reached by ritual is the single thing most likely to be mistaken for competence, and a scoreboard alone would congratulate it.

7The falsification drill — v3, thirty seconds

The one fast module. Three candidate checks, one claim, pick the one that would fail if the claim were wrong. This is the “think in opposites” training the literature actually measured, in item form.

8Real work — the verification record

The retention feature, and the reason the tool survives past the modules. Tracker never runs the check. No sandbox, no calculator, no search proxy — the learner checks outside and pastes back what they found. Building an execution environment would move the act of checking inside the app, where it stops being a habit they carry out of it.

9Progress — and the display that does the teaching

Composite, discrimination and ritual rate are shown together or not at all. The self-report overlay is the most directly persuasive thing the tool can put in front of a learner, because it is about them rather than about the model.

10RTL pass — fa

Mid-cost locale, and the reason is structural: fault classes are units, magnitudes, boundaries and inverted logic, so specs and keys are locale-invariant and only artifact prose is realised twice. No Persian-specific linguistic work, unlike Clarity's R4/R6. One inherited constraint: where a bench entry is an external source, the artifact may be Persian while the source is English.

What these plates are protecting
  • Cost before, outcome never (plate 3). The bench shows what a check costs and hides what it returns. A bench that previews outcomes measures nothing, because the decision has already been made for the learner.
  • The costumes are selectable (plate 4). Self-critique and stated confidence sit on the bench, cheap, and pay out nothing. A costume you're prevented from wearing is one you never learn to recognise.
  • Ritual before verdict (plate 6). When every check run was pass-either-way, the reveal says that first — a correct answer reached by ritual is the thing most likely to be mistaken for skill.
  • The element list is never on screen while checks are chosen (plate 5). Earlier, it's a shopping guide for which check to buy, and it contaminates V1 and V3, not just V5.
  • Free text, then the list (plate 5). The pick is the scored, offline, comparable component; the words are captured and judged only if a credential exists. This is what keeps V5 out of the credential's reach.
  • Probes are unassisted at every timepoint (§4a). Tiering the instrument between baseline and post would make a promoted learner look worse at the moment they advanced.
  • V4 unscored under a ceiling (plate 1), rendered as words, with the total reading 9 / 10 · 5 of 6 criteria.
  • “Cannot verify” is a first-class answer (plate 7), and inventing an oracle is scored worse than admitting there isn't one.
  • Tracker never runs the check (plate 8). No sandbox, no calculator, no search proxy.

Settled 2026-08-11, after review. (a) The discrimination line appears on every reveal, in all four states — none of your checks could have failed · some could · all could · you ran none. It is not suppressed after N sightings: the negative form should disappear because the learner stopped triggering it, never because the UI stopped saying it. A designed fade would hide a regression, and the fade this design wants is behavioural. (b) Six bench entries. A later variant may author more and display six — if it ever does, the six must be a fixed deterministic subset per item, never a per-learner sample, or the item stops being the same instrument for two people. (c) The cost-prediction field stays. (d) Western digits in both locales (see plate 10).
