AI Training Lab (hub) — wireframes · draft 1

Low-fidelity. Companion to 07-training-lab-hub.md. Eight plates plus an RTL pass. This is not a seventh lab: nothing here is scored, and no screen below collects anything. The whole design question is what one page says when it has six equal doors and no right to rank them — plate 3 is the answer, plate 7 is what happens when the numbers behind it are unavailable.

1The Tools page — six cards become one section

The other three sections on this page are heading + sentence + button. Skills was the only one that wasn't, and at six cards it dominated the page it was supposed to be one item on. The status line is the only new thing, and it is allowed to be absent.

2The hub, returning learner

Six cards, one of them wearing a ribbon. The order is canonical and never re-sorts by score — a grid that rearranges itself between visits destroys the spatial memory that makes an index worth having.

3The next-step card — five states, one ladder

First match wins; ties broken by canonical order so the card does not flicker between renders. Rule 5 deliberately names no lab.

4First visit — nothing started anywhere

The intro block is open, the due section does not exist, and every card reads the same. Exactly one thing on the page is emphasised.

5The lab card, annotated

Everything on it is either navigation or a count. Nothing on it is a score.

6Mobile — one column, same order

The recommendation stays above the fold; the six cards go single-column in canonical order. Nothing is hidden behind a disclosure that isn't hidden on desktop.

7When the numbers aren't there

The load-bearing state. This page's job is navigation; state is decoration. A hub that shows a full-page error and no links has failed at the only thing it is for.

8RTL pass

The grid is direction-agnostic. Two things are not: the secondary-entrance chevron and the next-step arrow. Both must be logical, not a hardcoded →.

Reading these plates
  • Plate 3 is the design. Everything else is layout. The five-rule ladder is what converts six equal doors into one recommendation, and rule 5 — which names no lab at all — is the one most likely to grow a bad feature later.
  • The hub shows progress, never performance. Six labs report metrics on six different scales; there is no arithmetic that ranks them. Counts of mastered modules are comparable, so those are what appear.
  • Absent beats empty. No Due-now heading when nothing is due, no ribbon when no rule fires, no status line on the Tools page when the payload doesn't arrive. Same principle as Monitoring's clean control revealing nothing rather than an empty block.
  • The doors don't depend on the numbers (plate 7). Titles and one-liners are client-side; the six links render before anything is fetched and survive an API failure.
  • Nothing here is scored. No attempt row, no rubric, no probe. This is not a seventh lab, and the six lab pages keep every metric, rail and banner they have now.
