Session rhythm, stage 1 — wireframes · draft 1

Module tracker/modules/05-session-rhythm/ · Phase 4 · wireframes. Low fidelity: layout, content and states only. No brand, type or colour decisions. There is no design step for this module (4b skipped, founder, 2026-11-19), so ux-review reads these frames. Every step of 03-journeys.md 0.2 is drawn here or listed in Coverage as having no screen.

Grading. Every frame is proposal. Captions cite journey steps (J1.7), spec sections (§4.2) and acceptance criteria (AC-15) from 02-spec.md 0.1. Anything drawn dashed in purple is a Suggested item from the journeys' §4 (S1 to S12): an option for revise, not a requirement. Calls these frames made themselves are W1 to W26, at the foot. Dates follow the journeys: switch Monday 18 November, first session Wednesday 20, the due-day session Saturday 23, the return Monday 16 December.

Drawn marks. the cross-out control (empty: not crossed; filled: crossed; dotted: none, "just to know"). the amber overlap mark. ⋮⋮ the drag handle. + in the top bar is the quick-capture control.

neutral notice success amber: overlap, not saved, refused (never red) Suggested (S-n), option only placeholder text

1Today — default, fold open, loading, empty, right to left, and desktop

Today opens on the list with nothing before it: no Organize or Review button, no wizard, no locked state (§4.1, D-64). One quiet session line under the date (plate 2), timed items first, "Any time" after, crossed items in a closed fold at the foot. No count anywhere, not even on the fold.

2Today's session line — the four forms, and the switch

One quiet line under the date, never a pop-up, never a number of days (§4.1, AC-13). The words are the requirement, so they are drawn in full. The link sits at the line's end; the line itself is not a target (W2).

3Quick capture — open, choosing a day, saved, refused, not saved, right to left, and a key typed in a field

Title, an optional day, an optional time, nothing else (§4.2, D-70). On a phone it rises as a sheet over the keyboard; on desktop it is a small centred modal (W8). Enter saves and closes; a short confirmation names the day.

4Crossing out on Focus, the actions list and the project page — linked rows, past-dated items, an old tab

The cross-out control takes the checkbox's slot on every surface (§4.3, [F §2.1 #1]). It is its own target, separate from the row's link: the control wins, so crossing never opens the item (AC-21). Progress on Focus and projects moves only at review.

5The session: Look back — first session, answers and follow-ups, a changed answer, "Don't ask", nothing to ask, not saved, right to left

/session is its own page: no app navigation, a step header, and an exit to Today that keeps everything (§4.4). Crossed items come first, pre-filled "happened", confirmed in one tap; then the rest by day, oldest first, each with its four answers. Every answer saves as it is made.

6Look back after a gap — the gap block, Not tracked, Fill in, and a session continued days later or in a second tab

When the range holds days older than today minus the spacing, those days fold into one block at the top with its two dates and nothing else: no count of days or items (AC-49, AC-61).

7The session: Look ahead — the next date, "From before", the days, "No date yet", moving, removing, loading, "From the gap", and desktop

Its own screen, not a mode of Calendar (D-73). The next session's date comes first, because the days below depend on it (§2.2 #6). Then the days, each a list in Today's order. Undated items wait in a closed "No date yet" fold above the days. Nothing is counted; overlaps show amber and ask nothing.

8The session: When and where — empty, filled with the calendar file, the place limit

Two optional fields and an optional calendar file; Finish completes the session and returns to Today (§4.7). The prompt's words are the requirement.

9Item settings — the review setting on the action, interval and routine forms; no estimate required

The spec places this on the forms (§4.8); no journey step opens a form, so it is drawn once, for placement. Ask me is the default; each choice has one line of help.

10Today's intro and the Concepts page — the copy that changes

No new screen: the existing intro and Concepts page lose every line that says Today is locked, and the pre-day and after-day intros stop showing (AC-7, R8). The new words are the build's; the frame shows what goes.

Calls made here

Behaviour these wireframes decided that the spec and journeys left open, each proposal. ux-review and build-plan's gate read this table: each call is accepted, refused or carried before the build plan.

#CallWhyWhere
W1The capture control is a "+" icon at the end of the top bar on a phone, and a labelled "+ Add" on desktop.§4.2 fixes the top bar, not the form; a label costs nothing on desktop and the icon is a common pattern on a phone.plates 1, 3
W2The session line's link sits at the line's end; the line itself is not a target.A whole-line target on Today's top line would be hit while scrolling and would start sessions by accident.plate 2
W3Nothing marks the switch beyond "Plan your next days": no banner, tour or modal.§4.1 says one quiet line, never a pop-up; the intro and Concepts page carry the explanation (plate 10).plate 2
W4A "just to know" row keeps an empty control slot and shows the words "just to know" where the project would be.AC-16 says it shows without the control and "reads as such"; keeping the slot keeps titles aligned.plate 1
W5The overlap mark is an amber bar on the row's start edge, on every row in the overlap, with an accessible label "Overlaps".An edge mark carries no count and no alarm; colour alone would fail for screen readers.plates 1, 7
W6Rows drag by a handle only, and a drop lands only among rows of the same start time or within "Any time".AC-11: a drag never changes a time; a handle keeps scroll and cross-out gestures apart on a phone.plate 1
W7Gathering is silent: Today and the look-ahead show placeholder rows while loading, and new rows slot in.Gathering is mechanics; a message about it would be noise on a surface that must stay quiet.plates 1, 7
W8Capture is a bottom sheet on a phone and a small centred modal on desktop.§4.2 says "small modal"; on a phone a sheet sits above the keyboard.plate 3
W9Capture's day is a row of chips: Today (shown selected by default), Tomorrow, the next named weekday, "Pick a date…", "No date yet". Time is a chip that opens an HH:mm field.Most captures are today or soon; showing "Today" makes the empty-date default visible rather than implicit.plate 3
W10Confirmations (capture, the .ics download) are short toasts at the foot with no link, gone after a few seconds.§4.2 asks for a short confirmation; a link would turn it into a navigation choice.plates 3, 8
W11No character counters. The title's 200 is checked on Enter with a message; the place field stops taking input at 80.§4.9 rules out counts on these surfaces; a counter is close enough to one to avoid. The title is refused, not cut, because AC-25 says refused.plates 3, 8
W12Escape or a click outside closes capture at once, even with a title typed: no "discard?" question.§4.2 and AC-27 say it closes without saving; a question would add a step to the fastest surface.plate 3
W13On Focus, the actions list and the project page, a crossed row stays in place, struck through; only Today has a fold.§4.3 names the fold for Today only; R2 wants what was cleared today visible on Focus.plate 4
W14Crossing an item not dated today shows "Moved to today and crossed out" once.AC-19 moves the date as a side effect; off Today the move would otherwise be invisible.plate 4
W15The four answers are four equal choices, two by two on a phone, one row on desktop; none is pre-selected for uncrossed items.No answer should look like the expected one; two by two keeps the labels whole at 375 px.plates 5, 6
W16An item answered "no longer matters" leaves the questions but stays as one dimmed line with "change" until the session completes.AC-36 and J3.8 change that answer back; an item that disappeared could not be changed.plate 5
W17"Done for now" is a link at the foot of the look-back at all times; when every item is answered it is replaced by a primary "Look ahead".R1: the way out of a long look-back must never need scrolling to find; one primary action at a time.plate 5
W18After "Don't ask about this" the row stays, dimmed, while the undo toast shows, then goes.Undo is easier to trust when the thing undone is still in view.plate 5
W19The empty look-back reads "Nothing to look back on." with Continue.§4.5 fixes that a line is shown, not its words.plate 5
W20The gap block is a neutral card with two equal buttons; after Not tracked it shrinks to one dimmed line with "change" until the session completes.A gap is not an error (no amber), and neither choice is the right one; a misclick should be reversible within the session.plate 6
W21A resumed session, and a second tab's Start, open at the first unfinished step with no "welcome back" notice.AC-28 and AC-29 say where it opens; a notice would make leaving feel like something to account for.plate 6
W22The next date is six day chips, today+2 to today+7, not a calendar picker.The range is six days; chips make an out-of-range date impossible to pick (AC-40, J5.4).plate 7
W23The look-ahead is one column of days at every width; its rows have no cross-out control, a "⋯" menu for move, time and remove, and a drag handle; each day ends with "+ Add to <day>" and an empty day reads "Nothing planned."One column keeps it from reading as a week calendar (D-73); crossing is about today, not planning.plate 7
W24"Off the shown days" means two choices in the move menu: "Later…" (a date after the shown days) and "No date yet".§4.6 says "out of them" without saying where to.plate 7
W25The session's header has "← Today" at the start and the three step names; a completed step's name is a link back to it until the session completes.AC-36 lets answers change until completion, so Look back must stay reachable from the later steps.plates 5–8
W26The cross-out control is drawn as a square in the old checkbox's slot: empty when not crossed, filled when crossed. The title strikes through.Made silently in draft 1 and raised afterwards: a box that fills can read as "done", against D-67. The founder chose to keep the box, 2026-11-19, on Claude's drawing, not after a review. ux-review may still raise it.plates 1, 4, 5 (legend)
