## Summary

1. **The canon's bet holds, and the no-AI version can carry it.** Reconstrual is the active lever, and the studies that show it caused it with written instructions, not a model. Distancing has a medium average effect across 230 studies [S8]. Expressive writing on its own has a small one [S5, S6].
2. **The intake's first move is the riskiest one for the person it serves.** "Story first" for someone who replays an experience is, as studied, immersed recounting: the route to rumination [S1, S2]. In the one trial with that population, people prone to rumination did *worst* with expressive writing and with narrative writing, and best with neutral control writing, eight months on [S3]. The story prompt has to be concrete and stepped back, not a re-living.
3. **Naming feelings alone isn't enough.** Writing only about emotions drifted more negative over four weeks. Writing that joined emotion with understanding produced the growth [S7]. Pennebaker's language work points the same way [S4, S9]. Feelings and needs need a step after them that re-reads, and the reality check is the obvious one.
4. **Tracker has already built the mechanism a no-AI reality check needs.** Noticing's `read` catch runs an authored lexicon over the observation field only, and offers rather than corrects. Feelings & Needs' faux-feeling catch does the same for feelings. The drop rule can get an instrument in the BSRI, a validated 8-item state-rumination measure, together with recounting-versus-reconstruing items and Journals as the comparison arm.
5. **The list of things to avoid is concrete:** immersed "deepest feelings" replay; abstract, evaluative "why me" questions; ending on feelings; repeated entries on one event; writing straight after an acute event; and an entry with no bound. The open question that matters most is how distancing reads in Persian, which this brief didn't reach.

## 2. Does structured, no-AI prompting produce reconstrual?

**Yes, in the lab, when the prompt sets the perspective.** Kross, Ayduk and Mischel cued people with written instructions to analyse a negative experience either through their own eyes (immersed) or as a "fly on the wall" (distanced). Distanced analysis shifted what people wrote from recounting what happened towards reconstruing it, and lowered depressed affect *(evidence, moderate–strong: [S1], [S2])*. Two meta-analyses put the effect size of distancing: g = 0.52 across 230 studies for psychological distance, smaller for self-conscious emotions (g = 0.29) *(evidence, moderate: [S8])*, and g ≈ −0.26 across 48 self-distancing studies *(evidence, summary only: [S8b])*.

**Writing itself can produce distancing, and the language shows it.** In two longitudinal studies, expressive writing raised self-distancing a day later, which predicted lower reactivity at one and six months. More causal words, and fewer negative-emotion words and first-person singular pronouns, tracked the rise *(evidence, moderate: [S9])*. Earlier, people whose causal and insight words rose across sessions improved most *(evidence, moderate: [S4])*.

**Average expressive writing is weak,** so a free journal is unlikely to do the job. Frattaroli's 146-study meta-analysis found a small effect (r ≈ .075). Reinhold et al. found no benefit on depression across 39 studies *(evidence, strong for "small on average": [S5], [S6])*. This matches the canon's §5 caveat.

**Distanced self-talk** (oneself by name, or as "you") improved regulation under social stress, at no more effort than first-person talk *(evidence, summary only: [S10])*.

→ *Proposal:* the canon's H6b holds. No AI is needed to reach reconstrual. What's needed is prompts that set a stepped-back perspective and ask for meaning, not detail.

## 3. The story step, for someone who spirals

**Immersed recounting is the rumination path,** and "tell what happened, in your own words" invites it *(evidence, moderate–strong: [S1], [S2])*.

**The trial closest to the intake's audience is a warning.** Sbarra et al. randomised 90 recently separated adults to traditional expressive writing, narrative expressive writing (a story with a beginning, a middle and an end), or control writing about daily activities: 20 minutes a day for three days, with follow-up at eight months. People prone to rumination reported the least distress in the *control* arm. Narrative structure gave no advantage. People not prone to rumination did about equally well in every arm *(evidence, moderate: one study, N = 90, read through the publisher's release [S3]; the article itself wasn't reached)*.

**Concrete beats abstract-evaluative, but only in the right sense.** Watkins' review traces whether repetitive thought helps or harms to the level of construal: concrete, process-focused "how did this unfold" helps, and abstract, evaluative "why does this always happen to me" harms *(evidence, strong: [S11])*. Training in concrete thinking reduced rumination and depression in a phase II randomised trial in primary care *(evidence, moderate: [S12], abstract only)*.

The two fit together. The harmful mode is **immersed and evaluative**: re-living it, and asking "why me". The helpful mode is **distanced and concrete-specific**: watching the scene as an observer, step by step *(proposal, reading [S1], [S8], [S11] together; [S8] finds abstraction alone doesn't regulate self-conscious emotions)*.

→ *Proposal:* keep story first, but write its prompts as **an observer's account of one bounded scene**: "as if you were watching it", "what was said and done, in order". Don't use "write your deepest thoughts and feelings". Keep "why" out of every prompt.

**Timing.** Writing immediately after a traumatic event can make people feel worse. Pennebaker's advice is to wait a month or two *(evidence, summary only: [S13])*.

## 4. The feelings-and-needs step, and the reality check

**Feelings on their own drift negative.** Over four weeks, people who wrote only about emotions used more negative-emotion words as time went on. Those who wrote about both emotions and their sense of the event reported more benefit-finding *(evidence, summary only: [S7])*. Together with [S4] and [S9], a flow that ends on feelings risks the "just journaling" outcome, or worse *(evidence, moderate, taken together)*.

→ *Proposal:* order the moves story → feelings and needs → reality check, as the intake does, and end on a re-reading, not on the feelings.

**The reality check has a tested relative:** the CBT thought record (situation, thought, evidence, a balanced view). In an RCT, a restructuring app and a defusion app each beat waitlist on self-criticism and distress (N = 87, two weeks). Defusion's gains were more consistent and ran through decentering *(evidence, moderate: [S14])*. NVC's observation versus evaluation is the design language, still thin (canon §6).

→ *Proposal:* frame the reality check as *noticing which parts are a reading* (decentering), not as *disputing the thought* (restructuring). That fits Reflect's "see, don't fix", and [S14] suggests it costs nothing in effect.

**Tracker has already built the no-AI mechanism.** Noticing's `read` catch matches an authored lexicon against the `observation` field only, has a per-type cooldown, and offers rather than corrects (`api/src/services/noticing/catches.ts`). Feelings & Needs' faux-feeling catch does the same for evaluative words wearing a feeling's clothes (`api/src/services/feelingsNeeds/distinctions.ts`) *(as-built: rishe-eco/tracker main @ 5fd71c9)*. Both rest on variation theory (canon §5).

→ *Proposal:* a Reflect reality-check catch on the story's text, using an authored interpretation lexicon ("always", "never", mind-reading and labelling words) in `en` and `fa`, built on the existing pattern. The project's note on Persian word boundaries applies.

## 5. The drop rule's outcome: an instrument

The drop rule is "one and two": drop if entries produce no re-seeing compared with journaling, or more caught-ness; drop if anyone ruminates more, or feels scored or judged.

- **State rumination:** the BSRI is an 8-item visual-analogue self-report, validated across three samples, and sensitive to an experimental rumination manipulation *(evidence, moderate: [S15], two independent records)*. It has no Persian validation that this brief found.
- **Reconstrual:** Kross and Ayduk measure recounting versus reconstruing with self-report items and coding of essays *(evidence, moderate: [S1], [S2])*. Linguistic markers (causal and insight words up, first-person singular down) are a passive proxy *(evidence, moderate: [S4], [S9])*. LIWC-style Persian lexicons were not researched.
- **Comparison:** Tracker's Journals is a free-text `JournalEntry.body` with no structure (`api/prisma/schema.prisma`) *(as-built: tracker @ 5fd71c9)*, the natural "just journaling" arm. Sbarra [S3] says the comparison has to separate people prone to rumination from people who aren't, because the effect reverses between them.
- **Tension:** the intake bans scores and scales *for the person*. An instrument is a scale. → *Proposal:* any instrument sits in the evaluation layer, opt-in, outside the entry, never shown back as a number, and it appears only during the trial. `spec` and `eval-plan` decide.

## 6. Overlap with Journals and Feelings & Needs

- **Journals:** free text that can be linked to a goal or project and shared by email *(as-built: `client/app/components/journals/`)*. Reflect excludes sharing, so it should not write into a shareable journal by default *(proposal)*.
- **Feelings & Needs:** a loop of body → feeling → need → optional small action, with passes never cross-referenced, a soft cap on passes "so it can never become a rumination inventory", a needs palette, and the distinction catch *(as-built: `LoopSitting` and `LoopEntry` in `schema.prisma`; `client/app/components/learn/FeelingsNeedsLoopPage.tsx`)*. The intake excludes the "act now" exit, so the small-action step does not carry over *(proposal)*. Noticing chose a needs palette of its own rather than reusing Feelings & Needs' twelve, a precedent `spec` should weigh *(as-built: `00-state-of-the-build.md`)*.

## 7. For the next phases

**Options:**
- **Guided recount** — the intake as written: story in the first person, then feelings and needs, then the reality check. The simplest, and the most exposed to [S3].
- **Observer recount** — the same three moves, with the story prompts set as an observer's concrete, bounded account, no "why", and the reality check as decentering. → *Proposal:* the recommended base. It changes wording, not scope.
- **Re-reading close** — after the reality check, one prompt that asks what the person sees now that they didn't. This adds a fourth move, and is a small scope widening, stated here. It isn't pattern recognition or a hand-off.
- **Interpretation catch** — the authored-lexicon catch on the story text, reusing Noticing's pattern. An add-on to either recount.
- **Ruminator guard** — before the entry: how long ago it happened, and whether the person has written about it before. Recent or repeated events route to a lighter, distanced-only version, or to a calming exit. This widens scope (the intake has no pre-check) and needs `spec`'s decision.
- **Evaluation pair** — BSRI before and after an entry, plus recounting-versus-reconstruing items, against a Journals arm, opt-in and research-side.

**Questions for `spec`:**
1. Guided recount or observer recount, and in which voice: "you", the person's name, or the first person with an observer framing?
2. How does distancing work in Persian, where person is carried by the verb ending and the subject is often dropped? Does it need its own wording, tested with Persian-dominant users?
3. Is the re-reading close in scope, or does the reality check have to carry reconstrual on its own?
4. How is an entry bounded: length, one scene, a time box? What happens when a person opens a second entry on the same event?
5. Is there a ruminator guard, or a timing guard for recent acute events? If not, what does the flow say to someone in acute distress?
6. Does the reality check use an interpretation catch, and whose lexicon (`en` and `fa`), on which field?
7. Feelings & Needs' palette and catch, or Reflect's own, as Noticing chose?
8. Where do entries live: a new model, or Journals? How does that stay unshareable?
9. Which instrument makes drop condition 1 testable, and which makes condition 2 testable? How do they stay off the person's own screen?
10. Is the comparison arm Journals, and does the evaluation stratify by tendency to ruminate, as [S3] requires?
11. Which node on the opportunity tree (`00-intake.md` §3)?
