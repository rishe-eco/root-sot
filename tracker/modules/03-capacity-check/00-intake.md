# Capacity check — intake

**Status of this page:** the founder's description, as given. Claims are **proposal** unless marked otherwise. No sources, no research.

## 1. Problem

The founder's journey starts from a need, not from learning: recognize a need, reflect on it, check capacity, organize, then learn what is missing. The third step doesn't exist. Someone who has recognized a need and reflected on it knows what is going on, but has no honest way to answer "how much can I do about it?", given their batteries, resources, skills, knowledge and abilities. They jump to a goal sized by the need or by guilt, not by what they can really do.

*The problem statement and audience are Claude's proposal, accepted by the founder. The founder could not find the earlier conversation about a "minimum battery", so this starts from the description above, not from a record.*

## 2. Pillar

Maintain, bridging to Organize (`ecosystem/canon/02-pillars/maintain.md`, `organize.md`). The founder describes it as Maintain "changing shape" from battery recognition into a capacity check. Maintain's canon forbids targets, intervals and goals on batteries, so `spec` must settle how a capacity read feeds a goal without making the batteries a target.

## 3. Place on the opportunity tree

Not placed yet. `spec` assigns the node in `ecosystem/ost.md`.

## 4. Who it is for

Someone who has recognized a need and reflected on it, and must now choose what to take on. They over-commit, freeze, or take on goals sized by obligation. They want an honest read of what they can do.

## 5. Smallest version worth trying

- **One capacity check, ending in one goal definition,** with no AI.
- **Input:** a recognized and reflected-on need.
- **A short structured read** of the person's batteries, resources, skills, knowledge and abilities, set against what the need asks.
- **An impact estimate relative to that capacity,** answering "how much can I do?".
- **Output:** a goal definition built from the three steps (need, reflection, capacity), handed to Organize.

**Out of scope:**
- Scores, percentages, gauges or any other quantification of the person.
- Targets, intervals or streaks on batteries.
- An AI that judges, mirrors or recommends.
- Comparing needs, or people, with each other.
- The Learn step (acting on a skill gap) and the Organize step itself.
- The need-recognition and reflection steps, which belong to Impact and Reflect.
- Sharing with other people.

## 6. What would make us drop it

> People finish with a goal no more fitted to them. It feels like scoring or judging them. It becomes a gate that blocks action.

*The founder chose all three of the options Claude offered: (1) drop if people end with a goal no better sized to what they can really do than if they had skipped the step; (2) drop if it makes anyone feel measured, rated or told what they are capable of, or turns the batteries into targets; (3) drop if people abandon needs they could have met because the check talked them out of it.*

**Testable:** no, because all three are self-reports with no instrument yet, and the first needs a comparison with skipping the step. `spec` and `eval-plan` make them testable.
